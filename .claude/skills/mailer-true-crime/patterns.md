# Patterns

## The Opening of Innocence
**When**: first pages of any piece. **How**: start with a childhood memory or a hopeful beginning (release, a wedding, a new job) from a loved one's viewpoint. **Trade-off**: slow hook. Make up for it with dread the reader brings from knowing the genre.

## Victim Braiding
**When**: before every crime. **How**: 2–4 sections of the victim's ordinary days, in their idiom and their family's. **Trade-off**: lengthens the build. That's intentional.

## Procedural Present
**When**: the act itself. **How**: killer seen externally; orders in quotes, in sequence; the victim's one small gesture; a cold inventory of the room (dimensions, colors, a brand name); blood in one plain sentence; exit; cut. **Trade-off**: can read as cold. The coldness is what horrifies.

## Letter Collage
**When**: the subject is incarcerated or separated. **How**: alternate reproduced letters with flat narration of the recipient's real life (new lovers, money trouble). **Trade-off**: long letters drag. Excerpt the peaks.

## Conflicting Witnesses
**When**: disputed events. **How**: give each version its own section, back to back, unresolved. **Trade-off**: reader uncertainty. That's the realism.

## Script Transcript
**When**: interrogation, recorded interview. **How**: a date and time header, SPEAKER in capitals on its own line, indented speech. Bracket it with sections showing the participants' strategy and reactions. Place the killer's own account of the murders well after the crime.

## Professional FID
**When**: police, lawyers, doctors, wardens. **How**: their career thoughts, tactical calculation, vanity, regret, in professional jargon.

## Register Shift
**When**: the case goes public. **How**: switch the chorus to outsiders and raise the diction's sophistication. Reproduce headlines and deals.

## Countdown
**When**: the climax (execution, verdict, final confrontation). **How**: timestamped sections from many viewpoints, maximum restraint, exact last words.

## Introduce Through the Meeter
**When**: any major newcomer. **How**: first portrait through the eyes of whoever meets them, with that person's suspicion, envy, or admiration.

## Witness Relay
**When**: the climax (execution, verdict). **How**: rotate 4–7 witnesses quickly, each catching one fragment, sometimes several in one paragraph. End on one witness's exact image.

## Quiet Aftermath (Coda of Fates)
**When**: the ending. **How**: keep going past the climax. A run of short numbered sections, one per survivor (debts, lawsuits, new lovers, a broken ring), ending on the most isolated survivor. Echo the opening epigraph. No moral.

## Short-Form Adaptation (blog post, podcast, YouTube script)
**When**: 1,500–5,000 words. **How**: 8–20 numbered sections of short white-spaced paragraphs; 3–5 viewpoints; one document insert (letter, script transcript, or datelined news item); victim braiding kept even in miniature; one-line sources note. For audio: speak the white space as a pause or sound cue and name the new viewpoint in the first words of each block.
