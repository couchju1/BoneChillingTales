# Ch 6: Rendering the Crime: Flat, Exact, Unbearable

## Core Idea
The murders get no rhetorical build-up. Each one is a short numbered section: the killer seen from outside, his exact words, the victim's small human gestures, and a cold inventory of the room, all in the flattest voice. Blood is stated as a plain fact, not dwelt on. The horror comes from banality and precision, and from what surrounds the act: the victim's ordinary last day before it, the wife falling asleep and the stranger who finds the body after it.

## Frameworks (as executed in the original)
- **The Last Ordinary Day**: the victim's final day is told from the spouse's viewpoint. A home project finished, a meal rushed, a goodbye with a small joke, the spouse picturing him driving off at the speed limit until he disappears from her mind. Tender, specific, no foreshadowing.
- **The Procedural Present**: one short section. The victim is described the way a stranger would see him (build, haircut, name tag). Then the orders, in sequence and in quotes, then the shots. The victim's one small human response (trying to smile while lying down) is noted without comment.
- **The Room Inventory**: at the moment of the killing, the narrative stops to list the room the way an evidence photographer would: tile colors and heights, floor size in feet, a brand name printed on a fixture, a broken seat. The coldness of the list is the shock.
- **Blood as Plain Fact**: one or two sentences, without adjectives of horror. How much, how fast it spread, where it got on him.
- **The Exit Echo**: the killer leaves through the space he came in, with the loot in his hands. The last phrase can echo the borrowed idiom with a near-invisible irony.
- **Cross-cut Without Comment**: white space, then the spouse at home: chores done, going to bed early, a faint sense of a knock that turns out to be nothing. Then the accomplice waiting in the truck, her own disordered mind rendered fully. Then the strangers who find the scene: what they bought, the exact address, a business card left, a phone call. Nobody connects the threads for the reader.
- **The Killer's Account, Later**: his own version comes much later, in an interview transcript, with an interviewer's reaction to how he tells it.

## The Execution: the Same Rules, Scaled Up
- Rotating witnesses inside the room (see Ch 3, Witness Relay).
- Exact procedure: cotton for the ears, the phone call, the hood, the target pinned to the shirt, the countdown that never reaches three.
- Blood rendered through one witness's eyes, ending on a specific object (the condemned man's shoes and laces). Exact, brief, unforgettable.

## Rules
- Exact numbers: time, dollars, shots, room dimensions, gallons.
- No sensory amplification, no authorial verdict, no slow motion.
- The victim's final moments are never sensationalized or eroticized. Don't invent the victim's interior during the act.
- The crime section is short. The surrounding sections (last day, cross-cuts, discovery, notification) run several times longer.

## Anti-patterns
- **Gore for thrill**, **ominous weather**, **"it was a night that would change everything."**
- **Hiding the facts**: euphemism is as false as gore. State what happened.

## Worked Example (original, fictional)
> It was 10:20 when the white pickup pulled into the Sinclair on Main. Kenny was by the pumps with the squeegee. He had a name patch on his coveralls that said KENNY.
>
> Wade asked him to empty the register. Kenny did. It came to a hundred and twelve dollars. Wade said, "In the back." Kenny went. Wade said, "Lie down." Kenny got down on the floor by the mop bucket. He was still holding the squeegee.
>
> The back room was eight by ten. Pegboard on one wall with fan belts on it. A calendar from Kolob Auto Parts, still on June. A drain in the floor.
>
> Wade shot him twice. Some of it got on his boots.
>
> Across the street, Loretta Fry was letting her dog out. She heard two pops and figured it was kids with firecrackers, it being close to the Twenty-fourth.

## Key Takeaways
1. Last ordinary day → short procedural act → cold room inventory → plain blood → cross-cut → strangers discover.
2. The killer is external, his words exact; the victim gets one small human gesture.
3. Euphemism and gore are both failures. Exactness is the rule.
