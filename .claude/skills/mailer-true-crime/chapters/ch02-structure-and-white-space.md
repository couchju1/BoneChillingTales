# Ch 2: Structure: Floes, Numbered Sections, Parts, and the Two Books

## Core Idea
The book is built in nested units. **Every paragraph** is set off by white space, like a detached ice floe. Paragraphs group into short **numbered sections** (1, 2, 3…), usually a single scene or viewpoint. Sections group into **titled chapters**, chapters into **titled Parts**, and Parts into two **Books**. White space replaces transitions at every level.

## The Hierarchy (as in the original)
| Level | In the original | Typical size | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book | Book One: Western Voices / Book Two: Eastern Voices | ~170–180k words each | Local world vs. arriving world |
| Part | 7 per Book, plain or wry titles ("Gary," "Nicole," "The Gas Station and the Motel," "Exclusive Rights," "Into the Light") | 17–36k words | One movement of the story |
| Chapter | Titled in caps: ordinary nouns, stages, or deadpan ironies ("THE JOB," "IN-LAWS," "A PROSECUTION," "STONE IN LOVE," "THE TURKEY SHOOT," "SEASONS") | a few thousand words | One phase or theme of days |
| Numbered section | 1, 2, 3… inside each chapter | a few paragraphs to a few pages | One scene, viewpoint, or document |
| Paragraph (floe) | Every paragraph separated by blank space | median ~37 words; 54% ≤40 words; 90% ≤~100 | One beat of action or thought |

## Frameworks
- **The Floe Method**: write each paragraph as a self-contained beat and set it off with white space. Readers move floe to floe, so each one should end on a fact, a line of dialogue, or a small turn.
- **Numbered Sections**: start a new number when the viewpoint, place, or time changes. A section can be a single sentence when the moment needs it.
- **Two-Book Architecture**: Book One is the private story (release, love, crimes, arrest, trial, sentence). Book Two is the public story, once the condemned man insists on dying and the world arrives. **Book Two is slightly *longer*** than Book One: the fight over the death outweighs the crime.
- **Relentless Chronology**: the main line moves forward. Backstory comes in through a character's memory or a document at the moment it matters (the girlfriend's whole history comes in its own Part after the killer's release).
- **Epigraph-and-Coda Bookend**: the book opens and closes with the same short verse, shifted slightly in the reprise. (Mailer wrote it himself and disclosed that in the afterword.)

## Real Proportions to Imitate (share of the whole book)
- Release and re-entry ("Gary"): ~6%
- Girlfriend's backstory ("Nicole"): ~5%
- The romance falling apart ("Gary and Nicole"): ~8%
- **Crimes, manhunt, arrest, confession: ~10%.** The first murder arrives about 20% into the book, after ~70k words of ordinary life.
- Jail, letters, the competency question: ~7%
- **Trial: ~7%**
- Death Row in Book One: ~5%
- **Book Two, the fight over the execution, the media, the last days, aftermath: ~51%**
- Final day plus execution ("Into the Light"): ~10%. Coda ("The Fading of the Heart"): ~5%.

## Pacing Tools
- **Compression**: a white-space gap can skip hours or weeks. Open the next floe with a time marker ("A couple of days later," "Saturday morning.").
- **Dilation**: crime nights and the execution morning are told nearly minute by minute across many sections and viewpoints.
- **Cross-cutting**: alternate sections between the killer, the girlfriend, the victim's wife, the accomplice, all on the same night.
- **Victim Braiding**: each victim gets their own sections (courtship, money, church, a home improvement, the goodbye) right before the crime.
- **The Coda of Fates**: the last chapter is a run of short numbered sections, each following one survivor's next year. It ends on the most isolated survivor alone.

## Anti-patterns
- **Thematic chapters**, **transition sentences**, **cliffhanger endings**.
- **Front-loading the crime as a hook**: the original spends a fifth of the book on ordinary life first.
- **Ending at the climax**: the original runs another ~5% past the execution.

## Worked Example (outline, fictional case)
```
PART ONE: Wade
  Chapter 1: THE FIRST DAY
    1  Darla, age 7, Wade takes blame for broken window
    2  Prison letters, reproduced in part
    3  Ron and Darla argue about sponsoring him
  Chapter 2: THE JOB
    1  Wade's first day at the tire shop (owner's viewpoint)
    ...
PART FOUR: The Station
  Chapter 12: THE STATION
    1  Kenny and Jolene's courtship, the duplex, the crib
    4  The robbery (external, procedural)
    5  Jolene falls asleep waiting
```

## Key Takeaways
1. Every paragraph is a floe set in white space; numbered sections hold the scenes.
2. Title chapters with plain nouns or deadpan ironies.
3. Put the crime about a fifth of the way in, and give the public aftermath half the book.
4. Close with a coda of fates and an echo of the opening.
