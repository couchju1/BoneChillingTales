# Ch 10: Method: Research, Sourcing, and Ethics of the "True Life Story"

## Core Idea
Mailer's afterword calls the book a factual account built from interviews, documents, court records, and other original material, and describes it as a true story with real names, told the way a novel is told. The novelistic texture rests on an enormous base of sources. Writing in this style is a research discipline first.

## What the Afterword Reports (the method to imitate)
- **Scale**: more than 100 people interviewed face to face, plus phone interviews. Something like 300 sessions of 15 minutes to 4 hours. Perhaps ten subjects on tape for more than ten hours each. The two key witnesses (the girlfriend and the mother) logged around 30 hours each. Transcripts approached 15,000 pages.
- **Chronology is motive**: a researcher found that each witness had characteristic recollection tics (merging or splitting episodes, misjudging gaps). Checking against a provisional calendar built from other sources fixed them. Getting the sequence right was crucial to understanding why people acted.
- **Conflicts**: where accounts disagreed, the author chose the most likely version and admitted he might be wrong.
- **Newspaper copy**: small cuts without ellipses, occasional transposition, only to avoid repetition or confusion, never to make it more absurd.
- **The subject's interviews**: trimmed and occasionally transposed out of decency to him, the way you'd tidy your own words in a transcript. Not improved.
- **The subject's letters**: the best ones, nearly uncut, showing him above his average so his effect on others is felt.
- **Confessed creations**: the epigraph verse was the author's own. One cross-examination was taken from a later interview and placed in a character's mind with that person's permission.
- **Name changes** for certain private people, disclosed.
- **Victims' widows interviewed** for the balance of the book, called the most painful and most valuable interviews of all.
- **Unseen subtext**: dozens more interviewees (prison guards, fellow convicts, a reform-school memoir, a convict's letters on the code) never appear but shaped the understanding of the subject.

## Frameworks
- **Source-Anchored Interiority**: every thought attributed to someone traces back to something they (or someone close) said in an interview, letter, or testimony. Otherwise, render them from outside.
- **Master Chronology First**: build a provisional calendar (date | time | who | where | source) from documents before trusting anyone's memory.
- **Interview for Idiom**: record subjects and harvest their phrases and the stories they repeat. That's the voice of their sections.
- **The Afterword of Method**: the only place the author speaks in first person. List sources, scale, conflicts and how they were resolved, trims, inventions, name changes, thanks to victims' families.

## Ethics Rules (for real cases)
- Don't invent events or dialogue as fact. Disclose every reconstruction, transplant, and invented element.
- Give victims and their families dignity. Seek their participation. Never invent a victim's private thoughts without a source.
- Rename non-consenting private peripheral people, and say so.
- Attribute contested claims to whoever makes them.
- For short pieces (blog, podcast, video, e.g. Bone Chilling Tales), end with a brief sources note.
- For fictional or composite cases written in this style, label them as fiction or dramatization.

## Key Takeaways
1. The novelistic feel is earned by exhaustive sourcing.
2. Build the calendar first. Correct chronology explains motive.
3. Disclose every trim, transplant, and invention in the afterword.
4. Interview the victims' families. They balance the book.
