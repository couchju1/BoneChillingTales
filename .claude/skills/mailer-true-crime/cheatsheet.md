# Cheatsheet: Decide Like Mailer Wrote This Book

## Decision rules
- **Want to tell the reader how to feel?** → Cut it. Add a concrete fact instead.
- **Need analysis?** → Give it to a character (interviewer, lawyer, psychiatrist) in their words.
- **Need to move time forward?** → White space plus a time marker ("A couple of days later," "Saturday morning.").
- **Need backstory?** → A character's memory at a relevant moment, a document, or a whole Part for a major figure.
- **Want to explain the killer's motive?** → Don't. Conflicting witnesses, his own letters and tapes, an interviewer's skepticism.
- **Simile or judgment word?** → Only if the viewpoint character would use it.
- **Thought without a source?** → Render the person from outside.
- **The murder itself?** → Killer external, exact orders in quotes, the victim's one gesture, cold room inventory, blood in one plain sentence, cut.
- **Interrogation or interview?** → Script format: a date and time line, the SPEAKER in capitals, indented speech.
- **New person enters?** → Show them through the eyes of whoever meets them, plus a brief biography in their idiom.
- **Two accounts disagree?** → Both, back to back, unresolved.
- **Story goes public?** → Switch to Eastern voices: outsiders, longer sentences, cosmopolitan similes, documents.
- **The climax?** → Witness relay: rotate a handful of observers, sometimes within one paragraph.
- **Reached the end?** → A coda of short numbered sections, one per survivor. End on the loneliest. Echo the opening.

## Viewpoint picker
| Moment | Best viewpoint |
|---|---|
| Opening | Relative who remembers him as a child |
| Romance | The lover, in FID |
| Victim's last day | The spouse |
| The act | External, procedural |
| Same night elsewhere | Spouse at home; accomplice in the car |
| Discovery | Strangers / customers |
| Arrest | Cops and prosecutor strategy, then the transcript |
| Trial | Defense and prosecution, alternating |
| Media phase | Producer, interviewer, rival agents |
| Execution | Relay: family, lawyers, priest, producer, prosecutor |
| Coda | Each survivor, one section each |

## Measured thresholds (from the full text)
| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Median sentence | ~10 words (Western) / ~11 (Eastern) |
| Sentences ≤10 words | ~50% |
| Sentences ≥30 words | ~5% Western, ~8–12% Eastern climax |
| Median paragraph | ~37 words; half under 40; 90% under ~100–120 |
| Paragraphs opening with dialogue | ~15% (up to a third in early, lighter Parts) |
| Speech tags | "said" ~68%, "told" ~16%, "asked" ~11%, others ~5% |
| Judgment words in narration | ~0 (≈2–3 per 10k words, all in characters' mouths or minds) |
| First murder | ~20% into the whole work |
| Trial share | ~7% |
| Post-sentence and media share | ~50% |
| Author first-person | Afterword only |

## Tells you've drifted out of the style
- "Chilling," "horrific," "senseless," "evil," "monster" in narration.
- "Little did…" / "What no one knew…".
- A transcript with Q:/A: labels.
- A simile no character would think.
- A paragraph of scenery no one is looking at.
- Every character sounds the same.
- Euphemism about what happened, or gore beyond the facts.
- A closing moral.
