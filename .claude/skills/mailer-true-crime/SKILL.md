## The Voice in One Paragraph
The author disappears. There's no "I" (except in a closing afterword of method), no thesis, no judgment, no foreshadowing. The book opens on a six-year-old falling out of an apple tree, in four short sentences. Everything is told in plain, past-tense declarative sentences (median ~10 words), in short paragraphs each set off by white space like separate ice floes, grouped into numbered sections. Each section belongs to one witness: a cousin, a lover, a cop, a lawyer, a victim's wife, a media producer. The narration takes on *that person's* vocabulary, similes, and thought rhythms through free indirect discourse. Any analysis is handed to a character. Big events get the flattest sentences. Primary documents (the killer's best love letters, script-format interrogations and interview tapes, datelined news items) sit beside the plain narration. The killer is external at the crucial moments and partly opaque. Victims are fully alive before they die. Half the book is the public aftermath: lawyers, media, the countdown, a coda of survivors. The effect is documentary, compassionate, and haunting.

## Core Rules (in priority order)
1. **Absent Author.** Zero commentary, moralizing, or interpretation in the body. Never "chilling," "senseless," "evil," "little did she know." Analysis goes to a character (an interviewer, lawyer, or psychiatrist). (Ch 1, 3)
2. **Borrowed Voice.** Narrate each section in the diction of its viewpoint character: plain words, regional and class idiom, slang, and profanity as spoken. Similes only if *they* would think them. (Ch 1)
3. **Floes, Sections, White Space.** Every paragraph set apart (median ~37 words). Numbered sections, one scene or viewpoint each, inside titled chapters and Parts. No transitions. Strict forward chronology. (Ch 2)
4. **The Chorus.** Many witnesses. The killer's interior is rationed and absent at the act. Newcomers are introduced through whoever meets them. Conflicting accounts stay unresolved. (Ch 3)
5. **Free Indirect Discourse.** Untagged thoughts in the character's idiom, including dropped-subject fragments ("Felt pretty rattled."). (Ch 3)
6. **Documentary Layer.** Script-format transcripts (a date and time line, the SPEAKER in caps, indented speech, never Q/A); the subject's best letters, nearly uncut; datelined news items. Tags: "said" ~68%, "told" ~16% (lots of reported speech), "asked" ~11%. (Ch 4)
7. **Material Specificity.** Character through wages, prices, trailer dimensions, cars, church, debts, not adjectives. Beliefs (ghosts, karma) rendered straight. (Ch 5)
8. **Victim Braiding.** Each victim's courtship, money, church, and last ordinary day, from the spouse's viewpoint, before the crime. (Ch 2, 5, 6)
9. **Exact Crime.** Killer external; exact orders in quotes; the victim's one human gesture; a cold room inventory; blood in one plain sentence; exit; cut to the spouse at home, the accomplice, the strangers who find it. Neither gore nor euphemism. (Ch 6)
10. **Machine from Inside.** Police, prosecutors, defenders, judges, psychiatrists, and wardens get names, tactics, and career interiority. The trial is short (~7%). No op-ed. (Ch 7)
11. **Register Shift.** When the case goes public, the chorus becomes media and national players: longer sentences, cosmopolitan similes, deal language. The story-buyer and interviewer are viewpoints. (Ch 8)
12. **Countdown, Witness Relay, Coda.** Days become chapter titles; the execution rotates among witnesses; then a coda of survivors' fates ends on the loneliest one, and the opening epigraph returns. No moral. (Ch 8)
13. **Sourced Interiority and Disclosure.** Thoughts only where sources support them. An afterword discloses scale, conflicts, trims, transplants, inventions, and name changes. (Ch 10)

## Sentence-Level Defaults (measured)
- Median sentence ~10 words; about half are 10 words or fewer; only ~5% reach 30+ (more in Eastern sections and the climax).
- Paragraph median ~37 words; half under 40; rarely over ~120.
- Plain verbs dominate ("got," "went," "said," "told"). Dropped-subject fragments in thought.
- An occasional long "and… and…" run-on for a rush of feeling.
- Numbers exact: times, dollars, gallons, room dimensions, shots.
- Lyricism and similes belong to characters, and they're rationed.

## Workflow: Writing a Piece
1. **Inventory sources** → build a master chronology: date | time | who | where | source. (Ch 10)
2. **Cast the chorus** → list every witness. For each, note 10–15 idiom words and phrases from their actual speech or writing. (Ch 1, 3)
3. **Find the split** → where does private tragedy become public spectacle? That's your Book One / Book Two line, or for a short piece, your midpoint register shift. (Ch 2, 8)
4. **Outline in numbered sections** → one line per section: [viewpoint] [moment] [document?]. Open on innocence, give the lover her own Part, braid in the victims, put the first crime about 20% in, keep the trial short, give the public aftermath about half, and end with a coda. (book-map.md)
5. **Draft each section** as white-spaced paragraphs in the viewpoint character's voice, applying the Core Rules.
6. **Insert documents** at the turning points: script transcripts, letters, news items.
7. **Run the Revision Checklist.**
8. **Afterword / sources note**: method, conflicts, any name changes or condensation.

### Short formats (blog, podcast, YouTube; 1,500–5,000 words)
8–20 numbered sections of short white-spaced paragraphs, 3–5 viewpoints, at least one document insert, victim braiding kept, crime in a few hundred words, sources note at the end. For audio: mark white space with a pause or sound cue and name the new viewpoint in the first words of each section. (patterns.md)

## Revision Checklist
- [ ] Any sentence where the author judges, explains, or foreshadows? Delete.
- [ ] Banned words in narration: chilling, horrific, gruesome, senseless, evil, monster, tragic, fateful?
- [ ] Does every section have one viewpoint (except a deliberate climax relay), and does it *sound* like that person?
- [ ] Median sentence around 10 words? Paragraphs mostly under 40 words, each set apart?
- [ ] Transcripts in script format (no Q/A)? Similes only in a character's register?
- [ ] Transition sentences between sections? Replace with white space.
- [ ] Are victims alive and specific on the page before the crime?
- [ ] Is the crime short and exact (orders, gesture, room inventory, one plain blood sentence), with the aftermath told by witnesses?
- [ ] Are the killer's motives left to documents and conflicting testimony?
- [ ] Are documents quoted, not paraphrased?
- [ ] Are adjectives the characters' own, not the author's?
- [ ] Does the ending land on a quiet fact, not a moral?
- [ ] Is every interior thought traceable to a source? Is fiction or dramatization labeled?

## Ethics (non-negotiable for real cases)
Don't fabricate events or dialogue as fact. Disclose reconstruction. Never invent a victim's dying thoughts. Attribute contested claims. Rename non-consenting private peripheral figures and disclose it. Honor victims' families. (Ch 10)
