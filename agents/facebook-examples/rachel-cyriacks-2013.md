THE BIRTHDAY CALL THAT NEVER CAME: THE DISAPPEARANCE OF RACHEL CYRIACKS
Rachel Cyriacks promised her mother she would call on her birthday.
November 14, 2013, came and went.
The phone never rang.
Her mother, Mary Schabot, knew something was terribly wrong. Rachel had always been close to her family, and missing that call was unlike her.
What Mary couldn't have known was that her 30-year-old daughter had seemingly vanished from the South Dakota prairie, leaving behind a mystery that remains unsolved nearly thirteen years later.
Rachel was a mother of three from Woonsocket, South Dakota. Her family described her as outgoing, fun-loving, and happiest outdoors. She was working to overcome personal struggles and rebuild her life with her children.
But behind closed doors, there were problems.
Earlier in 2013, Rachel had sought a protection order against her husband, Brad Cyriacks, citing domestic abuse. According to her mother, Rachel reported further abuse shortly before disappearing.
On November 13, 2013, Rachel drove her gray 1995 Chevrolet Silverado to Huron, South Dakota, to pick up Brad following his release from jail.
According to investigators, Brad was the last person known to have seen her alive.
He told authorities Rachel dropped him off at a friend's house.
After that, her trail went cold.
Weeks passed. Rachel never contacted her children or family. Her loved ones eventually reported her missing.
Then investigators discovered something unsettling.
Rachel's pickup truck was found behind a shed on a rural property outside Huron. It had extensive damage underneath.
Brad reportedly told investigators that he and a friend had been using the truck and working on repairs after Rachel disappeared.
But something else was missing.
A cherished quilt Rachel kept at home had vanished, too. Investigators considered it significant enough to include its photograph on her missing-person poster.
The truck had been found.
The quilt hadn't.
And neither had Rachel.
For years, investigators followed leads, searched possible burial locations, and questioned witnesses.
In November 2023, authorities conducted another search near the James River northeast of Huron. Investigators recovered items for analysis, although their significance was not publicly established.
The following month, South Dakota Attorney General Marty Jackley announced a reward of up to $25,000 for information leading to an arrest and conviction.
Authorities believed people knew what happened to Rachel.
No one has been charged in connection with her disappearance.
Today, her family is still waiting for answers.
Rachel would turn 43 on October 31, 2026.
And on November 14, another birthday will arrive for the mother who waited for that promised phone call.
Thirteen years later, the silence remains.
**Someone may know why Rachel never made that call.**
