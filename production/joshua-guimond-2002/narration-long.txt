=== HOOK ===

On the night of Saturday, November 9, 2002, Joshua Guimond sat down to play cards with friends at Metten Court, on the north end of the Saint John's University campus.

He got up and left the room at about eleven forty-five p.m., the sheriff's office says. Many of the people there didn't notice he had gone.

His own apartment, in Saint Maur House, was about three minutes away on foot.

The Stearns County Sheriff's Office says no one has seen him since. Investigators have named no suspects.

This is Bone Chilling Tales. This is the story of Josh Guimond.

=== MAPLE LAKE ===

Josh grew up in Maple Lake, Minnesota. He was class president at Maple Lake High School, and his classmates voted him most likely to succeed. He was planning on a life in politics and law.

His father, Brian Guimond, raised him Catholic and made sure he was in a pew every Sunday, WCCO reported in 2017. WCCO called that another reason Saint John's, a Catholic college, made sense for him.

By the fall of 2002, he was 20, a junior majoring in political science.

His roommate, Nick Hydukovich, was likely the closest friend he had on campus, WCCO reported. The two of them were in music groups and on the mock trial team together.

"He'd help pretty much anybody out. He didn't have any enemies," Brian told MPR News in 2017. "A good all-around kid."

His mother, Lisa Cheney, talked to him on Halloween, a Thursday. They traded emails the Thursday before he disappeared.

=== DEER OPENER ===

That weekend was the opening of deer season, and hunting with his dad was a yearly tradition. Homework kept Josh in Collegeville instead.

FOX 9, in its account of the Netflix episode about the case, reported that he went into his apartment at eleven oh six p.m. When he left for the party, he didn't take his wallet, his glasses or his car keys.

Forum News Service reported in 2021 that his keys, his glasses, his contact lenses and his wallet, with his credit cards and identification, were all later found in the apartment. He didn't take a jacket.

=== METTEN COURT ===

The party was small. There was alcohol. Those who were with him said they didn't see him drunk.

Accounts differ on how he left. Forum reported that his friends thought he had slipped out to use the bathroom. FOX 9 reported that he got up and left without saying anything.

The sheriff's office gives the time as about eleven forty-five p.m. The state's missing person bulletin says about midnight. The bulletin says he left to walk home to his apartment, and that he was last seen in blue jeans and a gray Saint John's sweatshirt.

About 15 minutes later, his friends called his apartment. No one answered. They assumed he had gone to bed, Forum reported.

=== MOCK TRIAL ===

The next day, Josh didn't show up for mock trial practice. Forum reported it was in the morning. FOX 9 put it in the afternoon.

WCCO reported in 2017 that Brian learned that same day that Josh hadn't been seen.

"I got home about eight o'clock and one of his buddies calls and right then it's like you get a pit in your stomach. I knew something was wrong," Brian said.

That evening, his friends reported him missing to the sheriff's office, according to Forum.

The state bulletin says family and friends consider it very unusual for him to be out of contact. It also says his car was found on campus, and it describes a four-inch vertical scar on his shoulder.

=== STUMPF LAKE ===

An organized search began on Monday, November 11. Deputies, members of the National Guard and volunteers went through parts of the twenty-five hundred acres of woods around campus, on foot and on horseback. Dogs were brought in.

Divers worked Stumpf Lake, which lies between Saint Maur House and Metten Court.

The sheriff then was Jim Kostreba. In those first days, his office focused on the theory that Josh had fallen into one of the campus lakes on the walk home. Investigators considered alcohol a factor. Several searches never found him, MPR News reported later.

=== THE CAP ===

Ten days after he disappeared, Brian, Lisa and Josh's uncle, Paul Cheney, sat down for a CNN interview in New York.

Paul told CNN that the bloodhounds had followed Josh's scent "down to the edge of the water, between the dorm that he left and the dorm that he was going to." He went on.

"They've dived three times. They've dragged that lake. They haven't found Josh. So that tells us he's probably not there and you need to move on."

Brian said a couple of deer hunters had found a hat with a Saint John's logo. Paul said the family had heard two versions. One was a navy baseball cap with a white S. J. U. logo, which he said was Josh's favorite hat. The other was a stocking cap. The public record doesn't say what came of it.

CNN's anchor said Josh was one of four young people who had gone missing in Minnesota and Wisconsin since October 30. On air, he read a statement from the Stearns County sheriff.

"We have no evidence to indicate the cases are linked or related. This investigation has been very active since it was reported first to us. We're involving anyone who may have any connection with Joshua. While we don't suspect foul play, it has not been ruled out."

Investigators later concluded that his case wasn't connected to the others, FOX 9 reported.

Lisa told CNN what she believed.

"I always feel that somebody took him. You know, we feel that somehow, when he was trying to get back to his dorm, somehow, some way, someone got him, and that's why we haven't heard from him."

=== TRIDENT ===

Brian believed investigators were too focused on the lake. "I couldn't even get them to put out an Amber Alert out. 'Nope, he's in Stump Lake,'" he told WCCO in 2014.

He told Forum News Service in 2021, "The first twenty-four hours are the most critical, and they just kept saying, 'He's in the lake.' Well, where's your evidence? They just kept saying, 'He's in the lake.' They just kept repeating it."

The family brought in the Trident Foundation, a group that investigates crimes involving water. With approval from the Stearns County Board and Saint John's, Trident searched all three campus lakes in May 2003. It found no evidence connected to Josh's disappearance.

Its executive director, Scott Romme, said afterward that the searches should steer the family, the community and law enforcement toward other avenues, Forum reported.

For two years, Forum reported, Sheriff Sanner held that Josh had not been abducted. In 2005, Sanner said Josh was considered a missing person and that he could have been abducted. Forum didn't say which two years.

=== FIFTEEN YEARS ===

Zach Sorenson, an investigator with the sheriff's office, was assigned the case in 2015. He told WCCO in 2017 that he looked at the file weekly.

WCCO asked whether the department had no reason to suspect foul play from the very beginning. He said, "I don't believe so. I wasn't there at the time. Just reading through it if it was a missing person at the time, it's still a missing person at this point."

He told MPR News that few leads were still coming in. "There's no evidence to point that it is an abduction or it isn't an abduction. Joshua went missing or was last seen at a social gathering on the north end of campus, and pretty much the good concrete evidence stops at that point."

Brian had his own view. He told MPR he was convinced Josh had been taken against his will. "It's like I said from day one. He was set up and grabbed," he said. He started a GoFundMe to pay for a private investigator.

Nick Hydukovich, the roommate, went on to practice law. He told WCCO, "Had there been some kind of accident like you hear about or still do hear about with college students he would have been found by now. It's always tough this time of year."

=== THE FILE ===

In about 2019, Brian made a formal request to the sheriff's office for the investigative file. The county denied it. In March 2021, his attorney, Mike Padden, sued.

"We feel that if we have that there's a reasonable chance we can figure out what happened to Josh," Padden told WCCO. He wanted the family's own private investigator to see the file.

The county said the case was still active. In court filings, it argued that releasing the records would "jeopardize the integrity of the investigation and could impede potential prosecution of anyone who played a role."

The case had a new investigator by then, Andy Struffert, Forum reported. He said he had worked through the file and drawn up a new list of tasks. Josh's computer had gone to the state Bureau of Criminal Apprehension, which was still going through its data.

Struffert spoke about suspects too. "We have interviewed a lot of people over the years, and I wouldn't say at this point there are any suspects."

Brian lost the lawsuit. Forum reported that a judge denied his request in May 2022.

=== THE COMPUTER ===

On October 21, 2022, the sheriff's office sent out a release.

"Several images were found on Joshua's computer after his disappearance. These individuals remain unidentified, and they may be able to provide information related to Joshua's disappearance."

There were 28 images. Steve Soyka was the sheriff by then. "These are people that we've exhausted all other resources to try and identify," he told MPR News. He said investigators didn't know why the photos were on the computer, or why they had been saved.

Four days later, Netflix released an "Unsolved Mysteries" episode about the case. One theory it raised, MPR reported, was that Josh may have been chatting with men online and gone to meet someone that night. Soyka said investigators had not ruled it out.

"We're open to all kinds of possibilities," he said. "A college kid typically just doesn't disappear from the campus."

Brian said no one had told him the photos were coming. He believed investigators had had them since the computer was taken as evidence in the early 2000s. "This is new information? Give me a break," he told Forum.

Sorenson, by then a lieutenant, called it "a strategic investigative decision to release these at this time to see if we could get those people identified as we haven't been able to."

=== SOMEBODY KNOWS ===

On November 9, 2022, the 20th anniversary, the sheriff's office said Josh's disappearance remained an active investigation. It thanked the public for the many tips sent in over the previous weeks. A year later, it said the same thing again.

By 2025, Sorenson told Forum, the office had received more than 200 tips since the Netflix episode, and it was following up on them.

Brian spoke to WCCO in the fall of 2022, a few weeks before the anniversary.

"Where is the body? Until then, he is alive," he said.

"All it's going to take is one person. One person to get a conscience. Somebody knows. There's people who know exactly what happened."

The public record doesn't show where Josh went after he left Metten Court. November 9, 2026, marks 24 years.
