On Monday, July 12, 2004, Troy Klug called his girlfriend, Duanna Beebe, again and again. She didn't answer. She was upset with him for buying drugs. [S1]

Later that day he sent her a text. He asked if her brother, Robert, was with her. She wrote back that he wasn't. It was the last she heard from him. [S1]

As the day went on, he didn't come home. She called his cellphone and left messages. After 7 p.m. his voice mailbox was full. [S1]

He was 26. In 2005 a jury convicted Tory Teigen of kidnapping him. In 2009 Teigen pleaded guilty to his murder and led investigators to his grave in eastern Montana. [S1, S2, S11]

## Tuxedos

Troy Jason Klug was born in Rapid City on May 27, 1978, to Ronald Klug and Anita (Joyce) Hinsch-Klug. He was baptized at the Cathedral of Our Lady of Perpetual Help, and received his First Communion and Confirmation there. [S14]

He was active in Scouts. He ran track and played baseball, and he loved soccer. His family wrote that he was "very light and fast on his feet and ran like a deer." [S14]

He hunted, fished, camped and hiked with his father and his friends. Most of the time he worked in masonry and construction. [S14]

His family remembered how much he loved getting ready for proms. The tuxedo, the flowers, dinner, the dance. [S14]

He was his mother's only son, the Rapid City Journal reported. [S5] That September, the Journal reported that he had never been embarrassed to tell her he loved her. [S3] His father died before him. [S14]

## The front

That Monday, Klug and Beebe's brother, Robert Highley, went to Cynthia Kindall's house in Rapid City to get methamphetamine, the South Dakota Supreme Court wrote. [S2]

Beebe testified that Klug wanted the drugs "as a front," so he could pay off a drug debt to Kindall. A front, she explained, is drugs handed over without money, paid back later. [S2] Kindall later said he had owed her money for drugs for two or three years. [S2] The 2008 opinion describes an alleged debt of $300. [S1]

Tory Teigen had been staying at Kindall's house. [S2] The courts spell his last name Tiegen. Newspapers spelled it Teigen. [S1, S2, S8]

Highley was asked to leave. He drove off in Klug's car. Kindall left soon after. [S2]

When she came back, according to the court's account, Klug was alive and Teigen had bound him with duct tape. The two of them put him in the trunk of her black Nissan and drove away. [S2]

## Cologne

Around 9 that night, Kindall and Teigen showed up at the house Beebe shared with Klug. The 2008 opinion puts it at 9:30, the 2010 opinion at about 9. [S1, S2]

They came upstairs to her bedroom. She asked if they knew where Klug was. Kindall said he had come to her house and she had told him to leave. [S1]

Beebe testified that the two of them went through his things. Teigen picked up a bottle of his cologne. [S1]

> "Well, if it's Troy's, it's ours now." [S1]

She pressed them. Kindall said Klug was no longer a problem. "Isn't that right, Tory," she said, and Teigen said yes. Beebe said they were smirking. [S1]

Just after midnight they came back. They climbed in through her living room window and ran up the stairs. Beebe was in bed, recovering from an operation, and her incision had become infected. [S1]

"Honey, I need you to come with me," Kindall told her. "I got to go away for awhile and I need you to go with me." Beebe refused. Teigen paced and said they didn't have time. [S1]

Beebe told them she had filed a missing persons report on Klug, and that she was probably under twenty-four-hour surveillance. They left. [S1]

## The garage

Between 6 and 7 that Tuesday morning, July 13, Teigen drove Kindall's car into the garage of a house in Belle Fourche. James Kusick lived there with Jamie Corean. [S2] The Journal spelled her first name Jamee. [S7]

Kusick testified that Teigen opened the trunk. Klug "couldn't see, his arms were duct taped, his legs were duct taped." [S2]

Teigen pulled him out and "threw him on the floor," Kusick said. He called Klug "a rat, a snitch," and kicked him in the stomach. Kusick heard him moan. [S2]

Kusick went back inside and told Corean that Teigen "had somebody with him." She did not suggest that they step in, the court wrote. [S2]

Teigen later testified that he put Klug in a toolbox in the garage that morning. [S2] Kusick said he "wanted nothing to do with" it, and he left for work. [S2]

Corean testified that after she went to work, she came back to see if Teigen was gone. He was. She locked the garage. [S2]

## The porch

On the drive home that afternoon, Kusick told Eric Haar that Teigen had left Klug at the house, and asked him to check the toolbox. [S2]

Haar opened it. Kusick, standing by the door, testified that he "could see somebody in it." Haar testified that he "only caught a glimpse" before he shut it, and that "there was no movement, no nothing." [S2]

Neither man testified that he saw Klug alive that afternoon, the Supreme Court later noted. [S2] When Corean got home, Kusick testified, they told her the man was still there. [S2]

Abby DeJong, Corean's best friend, came over around 6:30 that evening. She testified that Corean told her Teigen had put a person in a box in her garage, and that he was being "held against [his] will." [S2]

"I think we need to do something," DeJong told her. [S2]

> "No, the cops will get them." [S2]

DeJong tried to get her to call the police. "No, you're not going to," Corean said, DeJong testified. DeJong said that if she were the one in the toolbox, she "would want someone to help me." Corean said again that the cops would get them. [S2]

Late that night Teigen and Kindall came back with drugs, and everyone there used methamphetamine, DeJong testified. She asked Teigen, in front of Corean, why he was keeping Klug in the toolbox. Kindall answered "that Klug had owed them money." [S2]

## The trunk

Tell Cook also lived in Belle Fourche. He testified that Teigen and Kindall came to his house early on Wednesday, July 14. The 2008 opinion says 3 a.m. The 2010 opinion says about 2. [S1, S2]

They were going on about people owing money and about snitches, Cook said. [S1] Teigen took him out to Kindall's car and opened the trunk. [S1]

A man was inside, face down. His hands, feet and head were bound with duct tape. [S1, S2] Teigen punched him in the back and yelled at him to wake up. He made muffled noises. Cook believed he was alive. [S1]

Cook asked what Teigen was going to do. Teigen said they would either "go to Denver and turn him in or go to North Dakota and dig a hole." [S1]

Teigen and Kindall later said that visit came on Tuesday, not Wednesday. Kindall conceded she didn't remember the dates well. [S2] Cook said four times on the stand that it was Wednesday. He remembered because he was leaving that Friday for a race track in Denver. The jury had to decide. [S2]

## No water

Teigen and Kindall got back to Corean's house around 5:30 that morning. [S2] Later, Kusick testified, Teigen told him he had "woke [Klug] up," and that he was leaving. Before he left, he told Kusick to wash out the toolbox. [S2]

Kusick took it to a car wash. There was "absolutely nothing" in it to wash, he testified. "It had already been cleaned." [S2]

That evening Kusick and Corean were at the house with DeJong and Haar. Kusick said he "wanted to tell the cops." [S2]

> "We had a conversation that none of us would ever talk about it, we'd never say anything." [S2]

He also remembered hearing Corean say they needed an alibi. [S2]

Teigen came back to Cook's house on Thursday morning, July 15. He told Cook he had put the man in a toolbox and left him there all day. It had been hot. Cook asked if he had given him any water. [S1, S2]

> "No, I didn't give him any water, too late for that anyway." [S2]

Cook testified that Teigen boasted of going back to the toolbox several times that day to beat him. [S2]

That Thursday, Kindall and Teigen left Belle Fourche for Montana. According to Kindall, Klug was dead by then, and still in the trunk of her car. In Montana, she said, they drove into a canyon, and Teigen buried him by a fallen tree. [S2]

## Bleach

At 4 a.m. on Saturday, July 17, Kindall and Teigen arrived at a house in North Dakota. Jamie Keefe saw them there. [S1]

That night, Keefe testified, Teigen was cutting the fringe off a blanket while Kindall did laundry. He asked whether she was washing it in cold water. "Yes, I washed it in cold water," Kindall said. [S1]

The next day Keefe helped them clean out Kindall's car. The inside had been stripped to the metal frame, and bleach had been poured in it. Teigen told her he had used about three gallons. [S1]

She scrubbed the floorboards, which had a rust-colored stain. The rags were taken to a fire and burned. [S1] At one point, Keefe said, Kindall yelled at Teigen that the car should have been cleaned five days ago. [S1]

## Pound the pavement

Brent Gromer, a special agent with the South Dakota Division of Criminal Investigation, was assigned to Klug's disappearance. After he interviewed Beebe, Teigen was a suspect. [S1]

On July 20, Gromer and two other officers interviewed Cook. Cook said Teigen and Kindall had been at his house, but he repeatedly denied seeing anyone in a trunk. [S1]

He did remember Teigen talking about "snatchin some guy," and saying, "Could be in the trunk outside." He remembered the name Troy. [S1]

The officers didn't think he was telling them everything. Gromer told him to "pound the pavement" and "do every God damn thing" he could to find Teigen and Kindall. [S1]

On July 22, Gromer and Special Agent John Griswold interviewed Teigen in North Dakota. The next day he was charged with kidnapping Klug. On August 4 he was indicted. [S1]

Cook was arrested on August 6 and put in the Pennington County jail. [S1]

In September, Corean told Gromer that Teigen had been driving a red pickup that Tuesday morning. Earlier, Gromer testified, she had said it was the black Nissan. [S2]

## Kites

In the jail, Teigen was put in the cell next to Cook's. Inmates passed notes between cells, a system they called kiting. Cook wrote to Teigen, and Teigen wrote back. [S1]

Teigen called Cook "Chopper." Cook called Teigen "Mad Hatter." [S1]

The three of them were arraigned together on August 16. Cook testified that he saw one of Klug's family members crying in court, and felt the weight of what had happened. He suggested to his lawyer that he cooperate. [S1]

On September 30, Cook and his lawyer met with investigators. He told them about the man in the trunk, and he turned over some of the notes. A plea agreement followed. [S1] One note read:

> "And Believe Me they ain't gonna find No body unless some one else planted somebody. It may have taken me a while to decide what to do but once I figured it out, it was the best plan, and I executed it with 'grinding' precision! Don't worry about them finding a body." [S1]

At that meeting Cook said he had been "playing cop." Gromer made a remark about using "every trick in the book." [S1]

Teigen later argued that Cook had worked the notes out of him for the police. The trial court found that Cook acted on his own, and that officers did not direct that the two men be housed next to each other. The Supreme Court agreed. [S1]

## The verdict

Kindall was found incompetent to stand trial. She had a history of brain injury and mental illness, the Supreme Court wrote. She did not testify at Teigen's trial. Some of her statements to Beebe, Cook and Keefe were used instead. [S1]

Cook pleaded guilty to misprision of a felony, for failing to contact authorities when he saw a felony taking place. [S1] In July 2005 he was sentenced to two years of probation. Klug's mother told Judge Janine Kern that Cook could have been a hero, the Journal reported. [S4]

Teigen's trial began on June 20, 2005. Klug's body had not been found. [S1]

The jury heard Cook describe the man in the trunk. The notes were admitted. There was also DNA evidence, the Supreme Court later wrote, "strongly tending to prove" that Klug had been in Kindall's trunk. [S1]

The jury found Teigen guilty of kidnapping. At sentencing, the judge described what the evidence showed. [S1]

> "The evidence establishes that Troy was bound, beaten, detained in the trunk of a car in sweltering heat, duct taped. That he was then transferred to a toolbox where he was intermittently beaten until his death. He suffered terror, loneliness, misery and death. His body was destroyed and concealed. He was denied a burial...." [S1]

The judge sentenced Teigen to 100 years in prison. [S1]

## Sanctuary

Kindall was later found competent. [S1] In January 2007 she was sentenced to 20 years in prison for her role in the kidnapping and death of Troy Klug, the Journal reported. [S6]

On January 16, 2008, the South Dakota Supreme Court upheld Teigen's conviction. It found that one of Kindall's statements should not have been admitted, but that the error didn't change the outcome, given what it called "overwhelming evidence." [S1]

That year a jury convicted Corean of accessory to murder and of aiding and abetting aggravated kidnapping. [S2, S7] Kusick, Haar and DeJong testified against her. Haar and DeJong had plea agreements under which they were not charged and were given immunity. [S2]

The trial court found that Corean had provided her home "as a sanctuary" while Klug was held there. [S2]

On September 9, 2008, she was sentenced to a mandatory term of life in prison. She maintained her innocence, the Journal reported. [S7] "There is no discretion," the judge said. [S2]

## The plea

On November 18, 2008, Teigen was indicted for Klug's murder. [S2] He pleaded not guilty to alternate counts of first-degree murder, felony murder and two counts of second-degree murder, the Associated Press reported. He could have faced the death penalty. [S8]

The state later offered him a plea agreement that took the death penalty off the table. For a time he turned it down. [S2]

That spring, in a Belle Fourche courtroom, Teigen pleaded guilty to first-degree murder. [S2, S9] He cried as he acknowledged beating Klug over an unpaid drug debt and locking him in a large toolbox in 100-degree heat, which killed him, the AP reported. [S10, S11] He was sentenced to life without the possibility of parole, according to the Journal. [S9]

Rapid City Journal file photo by Milo Dailey.")

## Sheep Mountain

In May 2009, Teigen took authorities to eastern Montana. [S11]

That Tuesday he led investigators to a spot about 100 feet from where the grave was found, in the Sheep Mountain Range of Carter County, a prosecutor told the AP. [S11]

An assistant South Dakota attorney general said the remains needed to be tested. Personal items were found with them, including a wallet with Klug's picture. [S11]

His mother, Joyce Klug, had gone almost five years without knowing where her son's body was, the Black Hills Pioneer reported. [S12]

On May 12, the Journal reported that the remains had been identified as his. [S13]

Visitation was set for Friday, June 5, 2009, at Osheim & Schmidt Funeral Home. The funeral was the next afternoon. Burial was to follow at Mt. Calvary Cemetery in Rapid City. [S14]

## A raw deal

After his plea, Teigen testified at a hearing on Corean's request for a new trial. He said Corean "never indicated" that she knew about the kidnapping. He said Klug was dead by 3 p.m. that Tuesday. [S2]

Asked whether he was rewarding her for her loyalty, he said no. He said he felt she "really got a raw deal." [S2]

The Supreme Court wrote that he had essentially conceded bias. His plea agreement included an offer to Corean, and she was the only one who had not testified against him, the court said. [S2]

Kindall testified that during the kidnapping, Teigen had been using methamphetamine, had been awake for about a week, and wasn't thinking clearly. [S2]

The circuit court found Teigen's testimony biased and not credible. An entomologist, Dr. Neal Haskell, had found that Klug died on Wednesday, July 14, at the earliest. [S2] Corean argued that the entomologist had worked from a wrong date the prosecution gave him. [S2]

On November 3, 2010, the South Dakota Supreme Court upheld Corean's convictions and her life sentence. [S2]

His family's obituary gives the date of his death as July 15, 2004. [S14]
