At about 4 a.m. on Tuesday, June 27, 1995, Amy Kuns called Jodi Huisentruit from KIMT-TV in Mason City, Iowa. Jodi was the station's morning anchor, and she hadn't come in. [S7] Fox News later put the call at 4:10. [S6]

Jodi answered. She said she had overslept. [S7] She said she would race to the station and be there for the 6 a.m. broadcast. [S8]

It was the last anyone heard from her. [S8] Police found signs of a struggle outside her apartment building and believe she was abducted. [S19] She has not been seen since, and no one has been charged. [S3, S19]

## Long Prairie

She came from Long Prairie, Minnesota. [S8] Her family called her "Little Sue." [S9]

Her sister, JoAnn Nathe, remembers someone who liked golf, bicycles, boats and in-line skates, and who loved to travel. During college summers she worked the beverage cart on the golf course at Madden's on Gull Lake in Brainerd. [S8]

"She brought so much joy to the family," JoAnn told the St. Paul Pioneer Press in 2015. "She was just our sunshine." [S8]

Jodi graduated from St. Cloud State University. [S9] She worked briefly as a flight attendant before she went into news, Fox News reported. [S6] Then came TV jobs in Alexandria, Minnesota, and Cedar Rapids, Iowa. [S8]

## KIMT

She moved to Mason City two years before she disappeared, to anchor the morning and noon news. [S9] She usually reported to work by 3:30 a.m. for "DayBreak," which aired at 6. [S4] She hoped to land a TV job in the Twin Cities someday. [S8]

Doug Merbach was her boss. "She was probably one of the most personable people that I've ever worked with," he said in 2026. He said she gave viewers her time when she went out to meet them. [S16] Tim McCleary directed the station's weekend newscast. "She was always looking to have fun," he told Fox News in 2013. "I never heard anyone say a negative comment about her." [S6]

A police lieutenant, Rich Jensen, later described what a morning anchor was in a town of 27,700. [S8]

> "People here have a real connection with the local media. They would turn on the news, in the morning or at noon, and there she was. They didn't know her personally, but they knew her." [S8]

A few weeks before that June morning, she bought a red 1991 Mazda Miata. [S4]

## Monday

Two nights before she disappeared, she wrote in her journal about a trip she had just taken. [S7]

> "a road trip to Iowa City ... oh, we had fun! It was wild, partying and water skiing" [S7]

On Monday she played in a golf tournament. JoAnn suspects that's why she overslept. "Maybe she was just exhausted," JoAnn said. [S8]

That night, according to a friend, John Vansice, she came by his home. He said they watched a video of a surprise birthday party he had helped arrange for her on June 10. [S4, S5] He later told police he was the last person to see her. [S5] CBS reported that police have never called him a suspect, and that he denies any involvement. [S5]

## Daybreak

When Kuns couldn't get an answer after that first call, she kept trying. She told the Pioneer Press she called two or three times. [S8]

> "My first gut reaction was just to be mad. I'm like, 'Where the hell is she?' ... I thought she had just gone back to sleep and wasn't answering her phone. Never in a million years did I envision abduction." [S8]

Kuns produced the show and went on the air herself. [S8]

Steve Martinson was the station's sales manager then. "When I came in that morning we knew immediately it was serious," he told the Globe Gazette in 2010. She "was always someone who showed up for work." [S7] At 7:13 a.m., CBS reported, the station called Mason City police and asked for a welfare check. [S4]

## The parking lot

Jodi lived at the Key Apartments, 600 N. Kentucky Ave. [S7] Fox News reported that an officer got there at 7:30. [S6]

The Miata was in the lot. [S2] Around it, police found her red high heels, a blow dryer, hair spray and earrings. [S8] Fox News also lists her purse. [S6]

Lt. Frank Stearns told Fox News that her car key was bent inside the lock on the driver's door. [S6] The Pioneer Press reported that the bent key lay on the ground near the car. [S8] Stearns said drag marks were visible on the rain-soaked pavement, and that no blood was detected. [S6]

For years, news reports said an unidentified partial palm print was on the car. [S8] In a 2025 Hulu series, according to the group FindJodi, Sgt. Terrance Prochaska, the lead investigator, said the print was on a nearby light pole and not on the car. [S17]

Julie Birkedal covered it for the Globe Gazette. She wrote about what she saw beyond the officer keeping reporters back. [S7]

> "a pair of red pumps sitting on the ground near her car. When I went back later, a chalk line marked the place in the parking lot where the shoes had been." [S7]

## The van

Neighbors told police they heard screams. They took it for campers being noisy, Stearns said, because a campground was nearby. [S6] No one called for help, CBS reported. [S4]

Police have long believed she was taken shortly after 4 a.m. [S8] A 2026 report by KTTC says between 4:15 and 4:30. [S18]

On July 1, police held a press conference. They were looking for a mid-1980s white Ford Econoline van that a passerby had reported seeing in the lot that morning. [S4] Stearns said in 2013 that it was never recovered. [S6] JoAnn told Fox News that her sister's landlord reported hearing "two different male voices" in the lot and a loud muffler. [S6] Stearns said he believed one person was responsible, likely someone who knew her. [S6]

The fire department searched the Winnebago River behind the apartments. There was no sign of her. [S4] The search area kept widening. "Those first weeks we had FBI, DCI, not just investigators. We had teams of investigators," Stearns recalled in 2010. [S7]

## October 1994

Coworkers wondered about an angry or obsessed fan. Investigators searched her desk at KIMT. [S4]

Accounts of what had happened the fall before don't match. CBS reported that in October 1994 she said a black truck had followed her while she was jogging. [S4] JoAnn told "48 Hours" it was a black truck on a trail, a few months before she disappeared, and that it shook her. [S5] An ABC program, as the Globe Gazette described it, said she had spotted a van following her. [S7]

Stearns has said it never happened again. "We don't rule anything out, but I really don't think this is a case of stalking," he told the Globe Gazette. [S7] JoAnn told Fox News that whoever did this either knew Jodi or had stalked her, because the person knew her early schedule. [S6]

## Like a daughter

Vansice was more than two decades older than Jodi. [S4] Two days after she disappeared, KIMT anchor Robin Wolfram interviewed him. She told "48 Hours" what he said. [S4]

> "Jodi was like a daughter to me. She was just like my own child." [S4]

He said police had interviewed him twice and gave him no sign that he was a suspect. [S4] "48 Hours" crews came to Mason City that July and met Jodi's family. Vansice joined JoAnn's group and gave producers contact information for Jodi's friends in Iowa. "I hope she's OK and I hope she can come back soon," he told them, less than a week after she disappeared. [S4]

Months later, police confirmed he had passed a polygraph. [S4] CBS called him a friend. The Globe Gazette, in 2010, called him a man she was seeing socially. [S4, S7] In 2013, Stearns said he was still a person of interest. Fox News couldn't reach him for its story, and said he denied any connection to what happened. [S6]

## Tiffin

In 1998, a man who had been in jail with another inmate alleged that the inmate's rap lyrics pointed to a farm silo near Tiffin, Iowa. WCCO reporter Caroline Lowe and law enforcement took cadaver dogs to the site. The lead went nowhere. [S4] The inmate had lived in Mason City when Jodi disappeared. [S4, S7]

The next year, Mason City police put out a statement. After "interviews, crime laboratory analysis, records review, and polygraph examination," it said, he was "not considered, at this time, a viable suspect in the investigation." [S4] He has denied any involvement. [S6, S8]

## May 2001

Jodi was legally declared dead in May 2001. [S6]

Her family and friends started Jodi's Network of Hope. It has given more than $40,000 in scholarships since 2005, CBS reported. A granite bench sits on the 10th hole at the Long Prairie Country Club. [S4]

KIMT coworkers planted a tree outside the station one year after she disappeared. Photos from 1998 and 2018 show how much it grew. [S4]

## The journal

The tips kept coming, and some of them were strange. In 2006, a woman from Anoka, Minnesota, reported seeing Jodi killed. She later admitted she had made it up, and she was fined and given 30 days in jail for filing a false report. [S7]

In 2008, Jodi's journal reached a Globe Gazette reporter in the mail, with no name on it. It turned out to have come from the wife of a former police chief. The paper reported in 2010 that no explanation was ever given, and that the journal yielded little. [S7]

That year, Stearns kept a small plastic card behind a piece of electrical conduit near his desk. "It's Jodi's driver's license," he told the Globe Gazette. [S7]

> "I put it there to make sure I'd never forget. Now, I don't really need it to remember." [S7]

He said investigators knew no more than on the first day. "We don't know anymore today than we did on June 27, 1995." [S7]

After the 10th anniversary, she told the paper, she held her own service behind the apartments, by the river. "I floated three roses on the river, one for the past, one for the present and one for the future," she said. She said she feared Jodi's mother would die without knowing. [S7] She said she was convinced Jodi knew her attacker, and she declined to elaborate. [S7]

Imogene Huisentruit died in December 2014, at 91. [S8] "She so wanted to find Jodi," JoAnn said. [S8]

## The warrant

On February 28, 2017, Investigator Prochaska applied for a warrant to secretly put GPS trackers on two vehicles registered to Vansice's address. By then Vansice lived in Arizona, and officers had learned he would be driving to Iowa later that month. It was granted, and the file was sealed at the state's request. [S3]

In December 2018, "48 Hours" aired an hour on the case. [S10] Mason City Police Chief Jeff Brinkley said the warrant hadn't produced useful information. "That piece didn't pan out like we'd hoped," he said. [S10] Asked what police did have, he told Jim Axelrod they were "very close," and then, about how soon, "I don't think it's fast." [S5] Axelrod knocked on Vansice's door. He said he didn't want to talk and closed it. [S10]

Vansice died in December 2024. [S13] Attorneys working with a private investigator, Steve Ridge, asked the court to open the file. [S3, S12] The hearing was on March 3, 2025. The county attorney argued the investigation was ongoing and the affidavit held details the public didn't have. [S3]

Jodi's family put out a statement. [S12]

> "... we all agree that if the release of the information would hinder the investigation in any way, then the search warrant should remain sealed. We are not onboard with the information being released to the public, especially while the investigation is open and ongoing." [S12]

Senior Judge James M. Drew ruled on March 27. He wrote that the file showed Vansice "was considered a 'person of interest' rather than a suspect," and that the court had no reason to believe the state was keeping the case open "for any reason other than the crime is considered unsolved." [S3]

> "The court recognizes this is an extremely high-profile case. The high level of public curiosity is understandable. However, what the public would like to know is often very different from what the public needs to know or should know." [S3]
