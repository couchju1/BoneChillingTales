At about 4 a.m. on Tuesday, June 27, 1995, Amy Kuns called Jodi Huisentruit from KIMT-TV in Mason City, Iowa. Jodi, 27, was the station's morning anchor, and she hadn't come in. [S7, S8] Fox News later put the call at 4:10. [S6]

Jodi answered. She said she had overslept. [S7] She said she would race to the station and be there for the 6 a.m. broadcast. [S8]

It was the last anyone heard from her. [S8] Police found signs of a struggle outside her apartment building and believe she was abducted. [S19] She has not been seen since, and no one has been charged. [S3, S19]

## Long Prairie

She came from Long Prairie, Minnesota. [S8] Her family called her "Little Sue." [S9]

Her sister, JoAnn Nathe, remembers someone who liked golf, bicycles, boats and in-line skates, and who loved to travel. During college summers she worked the beverage cart on the golf course at Madden's on Gull Lake in Brainerd. [S8]

"She brought so much joy to the family," JoAnn told the St. Paul Pioneer Press in 2015. "She was just our sunshine." [S8]

Jodi graduated from St. Cloud State University. [S9] She worked briefly as a flight attendant before she went into news, Fox News reported. [S6] Then came TV jobs in Alexandria, Minnesota, and Cedar Rapids, Iowa. [S8]

## KIMT

She moved to Mason City two years before she disappeared, to anchor the morning and noon news. [S9] She usually reported to work by 3:30 a.m. for "DayBreak," which aired at 6. [S4] She hoped to land a TV job in the Twin Cities someday. [S8]

Doug Merbach was her boss. "She was probably one of the most personable people that I've ever worked with," he said in 2026. He said she gave viewers her time when she went out to meet them. [S16] Tim McCleary directed the station's weekend newscast. "She was always looking to have fun," he told Fox News in 2013. "I never heard anyone say a negative comment about her." [S6]

A police lieutenant, Rich Jensen, later described what a morning anchor was in a town of 27,700. [S8]

> "People here have a real connection with the local media. They would turn on the news, in the morning or at noon, and there she was. They didn't know her personally, but they knew her." [S8]

Accounts don't match on what happened in the fall of 1994. CBS later reported that in October 1994 she said a black truck had followed her while she was jogging. [S4] JoAnn told "48 Hours" in 2018 that a black truck followed her on a trail a few months before she disappeared, and that it shook her. [S5] An ABC program, the Globe Gazette reported in 2010, said she spotted a van following her that October. [S7] Lt. Frank Stearns told the paper that the incident never happened again. "We don't rule anything out, but I really don't think this is a case of stalking," he said. [S7]

A few weeks before that June morning, she bought a red 1991 Mazda Miata. [S4]

## Monday

Two nights before she disappeared, she wrote in her journal about "a road trip to Iowa City ... oh, we had fun!" [S7]

On Monday she played in a golf tournament. JoAnn suspects that's why she overslept. "Maybe she was just exhausted," JoAnn said. [S8]

That night, according to John Vansice, she came by his home. He said they watched a video of a surprise birthday party he had helped arrange for her on June 10. [S4, S5] He later told police he was the last person to see her. [S5] CBS called him a friend. The Globe Gazette, in 2010, called him a man she was seeing socially. [S4, S7] CBS reported in 2018 that police had never called him a suspect, and that he denied any involvement. [S5]

## Daybreak

When Kuns couldn't get an answer after that first call, she kept trying. She told the Pioneer Press she called two or three times. [S8]

> "My first gut reaction was just to be mad. I'm like, 'Where the hell is she?' ... I thought she had just gone back to sleep and wasn't answering her phone. Never in a million years did I envision abduction." [S8]

Kuns produced the show and went on the air herself. [S8]

Steve Martinson was the station's sales manager then. "When I came in that morning we knew immediately it was serious," he told the Globe Gazette in 2010. She "was always someone who showed up for work." [S7] At 7:13 a.m., CBS reported, the station called Mason City police and asked for a welfare check. [S4]

## The parking lot

Jodi lived at the Key Apartments, 600 N. Kentucky Ave. [S7] Julie Birkedal was a Globe Gazette reporter. She wrote later about what she saw there. [S7]

> "Beyond the police officer keeping curious reporters and passers-by at bay were a pair of red pumps sitting on the ground near her car. When I went back later, a chalk line marked the place in the parking lot where the shoes had been." [S7]

Fox News reported that an officer got there at 7:30. [S6] Her Miata was in the lot. [S2] Police also found a blow dryer, hair spray and earrings, and Fox News lists her purse. [S6, S8]

Stearns told Fox News that her car key was bent inside the lock on the driver's door. [S6] The Pioneer Press reported that the bent key lay on the ground near the car. [S8] Stearns said drag marks were visible on the rain-soaked pavement, and that no blood was detected. [S6]

Neighbors told police they heard screams. They took it for campers being noisy, Stearns said, because a campground was nearby. [S6] No one called for help, CBS reported. [S4]

Police have long believed she was taken shortly after 4 a.m. [S8] A 2026 report by KTTC says between 4:15 and 4:30. [S18]

For years, news reports said an unidentified partial palm print was on the car. [S8] In a 2025 Hulu series, according to the group FindJodi, Sgt. Terrance Prochaska, the lead investigator, said the print was on a nearby light pole and not on the car. [S17]

## The river

The fire department searched the Winnebago River behind the apartments. There was no sign of her. [S4] The search area kept widening. "Those first weeks we had FBI, DCI, not just investigators. We had teams of investigators," Stearns recalled in 2010. [S7]

## Two days later

KIMT anchor Robin Wolfram interviewed Vansice two days after Jodi disappeared. She told "48 Hours" what he said. [S4]

> "Jodi was like a daughter to me. She was just like my own child." [S4]

He said police had interviewed him twice and given him no sign that he was a suspect. [S4] Months later, police confirmed he had passed a polygraph. [S4]

## The van

On July 1, police held a press conference. They were looking for a mid-1980s white Ford Econoline van that a passerby had reported seeing in the lot that morning. [S4] Stearns said in 2013 that it was never recovered. [S6] Coworkers wondered about an angry or obsessed fan, and investigators searched her desk at KIMT. [S4]

In 2013, Stearns told Fox News he believed one person was responsible, likely someone who knew her. JoAnn told Fox News she wasn't convinced it was only one person. She said Jodi's landlord reported hearing "two different male voices" in the lot at that time and a loud muffler. [S6]

One year after she disappeared, her coworkers planted a memorial tree outside the station. [S4]

## Tiffin

In 1998, a former jail mate of a man who had lived in Mason City when Jodi disappeared alleged that the man's rap lyrics pointed to a farm silo near Tiffin, Iowa. WCCO reporter Caroline Lowe and law enforcement took cadaver dogs to the site. The lead went nowhere. [S4, S7]

The next year, Mason City police put out a statement. After "interviews, crime laboratory analysis, records review, and polygraph examination," it said, he was "not considered, at this time, a viable suspect in the investigation." [S4] He has denied any involvement. [S6, S8]

## Three roses

Jodi was legally declared dead in May 2001. [S6]

Her family and friends started Jodi's Network of Hope. It has given more than $40,000 in scholarships since 2005, CBS reported. In her memory, it placed a granite bench on the 10th hole at the Long Prairie Country Club. [S4]

Kuns told the Globe Gazette in 2010 that she held her own service after the 10th anniversary, behind the apartments, by the river. "I floated three roses on the river, one for the past, one for the present and one for the future," she said. She said she feared Jodi's mother would die without knowing. [S7]

In 2006, a woman from Anoka, Minnesota, reported seeing Jodi killed. She later admitted she had made it up, and she was fined and given 30 days in jail for filing a false report. [S7]

In 2008, Jodi's journal reached a Globe Gazette reporter, sent anonymously. The paper reported in 2010 that it yielded little information. [S7]

In 2010, Stearns kept a small plastic card behind a piece of electrical conduit near his desk. "It's Jodi's driver's license," he told the Globe Gazette. [S7]

> "I put it there to make sure I'd never forget. Now, I don't really need it to remember." [S7]

"We don't know anymore today than we did on June 27, 1995," he said. [S7]

Imogene Huisentruit, Jodi's mother, died in December 2014, at 91. "She so wanted to find Jodi," JoAnn said. [S8]

## The warrant

On February 28, 2017, Investigator Prochaska applied for a warrant to "surreptitiously" put GPS trackers on two vehicles registered to Vansice's address. By then Vansice lived in Arizona, and officers had learned he would be driving to Iowa later that month. It was granted, and the file was sealed at the state's request. [S3]

Mason City Police Chief Jeff Brinkley told "48 Hours" in 2018 that the warrant hadn't produced useful information. "That piece didn't pan out like we'd hoped," he said. [S10]

## Billboards

On June 5, 2018, Jodi's 50th birthday, FindJodi dedicated billboards around Mason City. They carried her photo and the words "Somebody knows something...is it YOU?" [S10]

That December, "48 Hours" aired an hour on the case. [S10] "We have never closed the case," Brinkley said. "It's never been a cold case for us." He said, "I'm not ready to quit yet." [S5] Afterward, he said the department received at least two dozen calls and emails. [S10]

## Winsted

In October 2024, Mason City police went to Winsted, Minnesota, west of the Twin Cities, on an old lead, according to FindJodi. [S15, S17] Winsted police confirmed "no human remains" were found, according to FindJodi. [S15]

## The hearing

On March 3, 2025, a court heard a motion by attorneys working with a private investigator, Steve Ridge, to open the 2017 warrant file. [S3, S12] Vansice had died the previous December. [S13] The county attorney argued the investigation was ongoing and the affidavit held details the public didn't have. [S3]

Jodi's family put out a statement. [S12]

> "... we all agree that if the release of the information would hinder the investigation in any way, then the search warrant should remain sealed. We are not onboard with the information being released to the public, especially while the investigation is open and ongoing." [S12]

Senior Judge James M. Drew ruled on March 27. He wrote that the file showed Vansice "was considered a 'person of interest' rather than a suspect," and that the court had no reason to believe the state was keeping the case open "for any reason other than the crime is considered unsolved." [S3]

> "The court recognizes this is an extremely high-profile case. The high level of public curiosity is understandable. However, what the public would like to know is often very different from what the public needs to know or should know." [S3]

The application, the warrant and the return could be opened. The affidavit stayed sealed. [S3] Ridge appealed, and KAAL reported in December 2025 that he had withdrawn the appeal. "At this point we need to trust the system to work on behalf of justice for Jodi," he said. [S14]
