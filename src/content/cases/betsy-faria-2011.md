## Who Betsy Faria was

Betsy Faria worked in a State Farm office and had a side job as a DJ. She had two teenage daughters and was married to Russ Faria. [S2] She was 42. [S1]

She met Pam Hupp at the State Farm office. Hupp was a coworker, and Betsy was the first person she met there. [S2]

In January 2010, Betsy was diagnosed with breast cancer. By October 2011 it had spread to her liver. [S2]

## The insurance policy

In December 2011, a change-of-beneficiary form made Hupp the sole beneficiary of Betsy's life insurance. [S2] The policy was worth $150,000. Russ remained the beneficiary of a separate $100,000 policy. [S1]

The Exoneration Registry says the change came three days before Betsy died. [S1] NBC News reported it as four days, and said no other friend or family member knew about it. [S3]

Hupp later said she had put $100,000 into a trust for Betsy's daughters. In a deposition in a lawsuit the daughters brought, she admitted she had kept all of the money herself. [S1]

## December 27, 2011

That day, Hupp drove Betsy to chemotherapy at Siteman Cancer Center, then drove her home to Troy. [S2] Hupp told police she dropped Betsy off at about 7 p.m. [S1]

At 7:04 p.m., Hupp called her husband, Mark, from near the Faria home. [S1] Betsy's last known contact with anyone was a voicemail left for Mark Hupp around 7:05 p.m. [S2] Phone records later placed Hupp still in Troy at 7:27 p.m. [S2]

Russ said he had left home at about 5:30 p.m. and driven to Lake Saint Louis to watch a movie with friends. He said he left around 9 p.m., bought a sandwich at an Arby's and drove about 25 miles home. A receipt and surveillance video backed up the Arby's stop. [S1]

At 9:40 p.m., Russ called 911. He had found Betsy dead. [S1] She had been stabbed more than 50 times, and the knife was still in her neck. Her body was cold, which meant she had been dead for at least a couple of hours. [S1]

## The case against Russ Faria

Police found Russ's bedroom slippers, smeared with Betsy's blood, in his closet. They arrested him on January 4, 2012, after Betsy's funeral, and charged him with first-degree murder and armed criminal action. [S1]

He went on trial in Lincoln County in November 2013. The judge kept out cell phone evidence that suggested Hupp stayed near the house for up to 30 minutes after she said she left. A 911 supervisor testified, over the defense's objection, that Russ's demeanor on the call seemed staged. [S1]

On November 21, 2013, the jury convicted Russ of first-degree murder and armed criminal action. He was sentenced to life without parole. [S1]

## Pam Hupp's changing accounts

In February 2014, the St. Louis Post-Dispatch and KTVI Fox 2 published an investigation into Hupp's shifting accounts of that night. [S1] The 911 operator who took Russ's call told reporters he had been genuinely distraught. [S1]

According to St. Louis Magazine's review of the record: [S2]

- Hupp first said she didn't go inside the house. Later she said she "just went in, turned on hall light." Later still, she said she went to the bedroom.
- She said she called Betsy from the highway, but phone records placed her still in Troy at 7:27 p.m. She then said she had pulled over at a fork in the road.
- In 2015, she said she now remembered a "silver-ish" car with two men near the house, and that one of them was Russ.
- She refused to take a polygraph, citing medical reasons. Her doctor said no condition would prevent her from taking one.

## A new trial

In June 2015, Circuit Judge Steven Ohmer granted Russ a new trial, based on the evidence about Hupp. Russ was released on bond on June 15, 2015. [S1] He had spent three and a half years in prison. [S6]

Before the retrial, the defense found more than 100 crime scene photographs police had never turned over. [S1]

Russ chose to have a judge, not a jury, decide the case. Hupp was not called as a witness. Detectives testified about her contradictory statements. They also said that three years after the murder, she said for the first time that she'd had a sexual relationship with Betsy, and that Russ had found out and was angry. [S1]

On November 6, 2015, Judge Ohmer acquitted Russ of both charges. [S1]

In July 2016, Russ sued Lincoln County in federal court. The suit settled in March 2020 for $2 million. [S1] [S4]

## Louis Gumpenberger

On August 10, 2016, a woman in St. Charles County called 911 about a strange encounter. She said a woman in a dark SUV had pulled up, claimed to be a producer for NBC's Dateline, and offered her $1,000 to record a sound bite about 911 calls. When the driver couldn't show any credentials, the woman asked to be taken home. [S3]

Surveillance video showed the SUV's license plate matched one registered to Hupp, and the woman later picked Hupp out of a photo lineup. [S3] Dateline said it had never offered to pay anyone for a sound bite and was not working on a story about 911 calls. [S3]

Six days later, on August 16, 2016, Hupp called 911 at 12:08 p.m. She said someone was breaking into her home in O'Fallon and that she had shot him. [S3] The man was Louis Gumpenberger, 33. [S3] He had mental and physical disabilities from an earlier accident. [S4]

Hupp told police that Gumpenberger, whom she described as a stranger, held a knife on her and demanded she take him to a bank "to get Russ's money." She said she got a gun and shot him. [S4]

Police found $900 and a handwritten note in his pockets. [S3] According to the probable cause statement, the note appeared to tell him to kidnap Hupp, get Russ Faria's money from her bank, and kill her to collect the rest of $10,000. The name "Faria" appeared on it. [S3] Four of the $100 bills he carried had serial numbers in sequence with a $100 bill found in Hupp's possession. [S2]

Prosecutors said Hupp had lured Gumpenberger to her house by posing as a Dateline producer, and that she was trying to make it look as if Russ had hired a hit man to kill her. [S3] [S4] On August 23, 2016, she was charged with first-degree murder and armed criminal action. [S3]

In June 2019, Hupp entered an Alford plea. Under an Alford plea, a defendant does not admit guilt but agrees the evidence is strong enough to convict. She was sentenced to life without parole. [S1]

## Pam Hupp is charged

In July 2021, Lincoln County Prosecuting Attorney Mike Wood charged Hupp with first-degree murder in Betsy's death. [S1] [S4] Prosecutors allege she killed Betsy for the life insurance money and staged the scene to make Russ look guilty. [S4] Hupp has pleaded not guilty. [S5]

The case had been moved to Springfield, about four hours from Lincoln County. In October 2023, Wood dropped and refiled the charge so he could ask for a trial closer to home. He said the long trip was "too much to ask of witnesses" and of Betsy's family. [S4]

## Where it stands

In April 2026, Hupp agreed to give up her right to a jury trial. In exchange, prosecutors agreed not to seek the death penalty. Russ has said he supports the decision. [S5] A judge is set to hear the case at a bench trial starting January 31, 2028. [S5]

In mid-September 2026, Hupp's lawyers asked the court to keep out all evidence about Louis Gumpenberger's death. [S6] Joel Schwartz, the lawyer who won Russ's acquittal, said he expects prosecutors to argue it shows "consciousness of guilt." A hearing on the request is set for October 19, 2026. [S6]

"This is going to be a bench trial, so the judge hears everything," Russ said. He is waiting to testify, which he said he hasn't done in 15 years. [S6]

Hupp has not been tried for Betsy's death. No one stands convicted of killing her. [S5]
