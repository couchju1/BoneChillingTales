At 7:27 p.m. on December 27, 2011, Pam Hupp's cell phone was still in Troy, Missouri. [S2]

She would later say she had already been on the highway by then, calling her friend from the road. The phone records said otherwise. [S2]

About two hours later, Russ Faria came home and found his wife, Betsy, dead. He called 911 at 9:40. [S1]

Eight days later, police arrested him for her murder. [S1] More than three years would pass before a judge weighed what that phone record meant. [S1]

## Betsy

Betsy Faria worked in a State Farm office. On the side, she worked as a DJ. [S2]

She was 42, married to Russ, and the mother of two teenage daughters. [S1] [S2]

When Pam Hupp came to work at that office, Betsy was the first person she met. [S2]

Doctors diagnosed Betsy with breast cancer in January 2010. By October 2011, it had spread to her liver. [S2] She was still going to chemotherapy that December. [S2]

That same month, a change-of-beneficiary form made Pam Hupp the sole beneficiary of Betsy's $150,000 life insurance policy. [S1] [S2] Russ remained the beneficiary of a separate $100,000 policy. [S1]

The sources disagree on the timing. The National Registry of Exonerations puts the change three days before Betsy died. [S1] NBC News reported four days, and said no other friend or family member knew about it. [S3]

Hupp would later say she put $100,000 of that money into a trust for Betsy's daughters. [S1]

Either way, Betsy had only days left.

## Two days after Christmas

Betsy had chemotherapy at Siteman Cancer Center that day. Pam Hupp drove her home to Troy afterward. [S2]

Russ wasn't there. He had left around 5:30 p.m. to watch a movie with friends in Lake Saint Louis. [S1]

Here is what the record holds for the next half hour.

Hupp told police she dropped Betsy off at about 7 p.m. [S1]

At 7:04, Hupp called her husband, Mark, from near the Faria home. [S1]

Around 7:05, Betsy left a voicemail for Mark Hupp. It is the last known contact she had with anyone. [S2]

At 7:27, Hupp's phone was still in Troy. [S2]

Then the record goes quiet.

Russ said he left Lake Saint Louis around 9 p.m. He stopped at an Arby's for a sandwich and drove about 25 miles home. A receipt and surveillance video backed him up. [S1]

At 9:40, he called 911. Betsy had been stabbed to death. Her body was already cold, which meant she had been dead for at least a couple of hours. [S1]

Russ had a receipt, a surveillance video, and an evening spent with friends. None of it kept him out of jail.

## The slippers

Police found Russ's bedroom slippers in his closet, smeared with Betsy's blood. They arrested him on January 4, 2012, after her funeral. He was charged with first-degree murder and armed criminal action. [S1]

His trial opened in Lincoln County in November 2013. Two rulings shaped it. [S1]

The judge kept out cell phone evidence suggesting that Hupp had stayed near the house for up to 30 minutes after she said she left. And over the defense's objection, a 911 supervisor testified that Russ's demeanor on the call seemed staged. [S1]

The jury convicted Russ on November 21, 2013. He was sentenced to life without parole. [S1]

The jury never heard the cell phone evidence about where Pam Hupp had been.

## The stories change

Within three months, the St. Louis Post-Dispatch and KTVI Fox 2 published an investigation into Hupp's shifting accounts of that night. [S1]

The 911 operator who actually took Russ's call told reporters that Russ had been genuinely distraught. [S1]

Hupp's own story, laid out side by side in the record, kept moving. [S2]

First, she said she never went inside the house. Then she said she "just went in, turned on hall light." Later, she said she went as far as the bedroom. [S2]

She said she had called Betsy from the highway. When phone records placed her in Troy at 7:27, she said she had pulled over at a fork in the road. [S2]

By 2015, she remembered something new: a "silver-ish" car parked near the house with two men inside. One of them, she said, was Russ. [S2]

She declined a polygraph, citing medical reasons. Her doctor said no condition would have kept her from taking one. [S2]

## Free

Circuit Judge Steven Ohmer granted Russ a new trial in June 2015, based on the evidence about Hupp. Russ walked out on bond on June 15. [S1] He had spent three and a half years in prison. [S6]

Before the retrial, his defense team found more than 100 crime scene photographs that police had never turned over. [S1]

This time Russ chose to have a judge decide the case instead of a jury. Hupp was not called to testify. [S1]

Detectives described her contradictory statements, and one more. Three years after the murder, Hupp said for the first time that she and Betsy had been in a sexual relationship, and that Russ had found out and was angry. [S1]

Judge Ohmer acquitted Russ of both charges on November 6, 2015. [S1] Russ later sued Lincoln County, and the suit settled for $2 million. [S1] [S4]

For nine months, that looked like the end of the story.

## The producer

The woman in the dark SUV said she was a producer for NBC's Dateline. [S3]

She had pulled up beside a woman in St. Charles County and offered her $1,000 to record a sound bite about 911 calls. When the driver couldn't show any credentials, the woman asked to be taken home. On August 10, 2016, she called 911 to report it. [S3]

Surveillance video showed that the SUV's license plate matched one registered to Pam Hupp. The woman later picked Hupp out of a photo lineup. [S3]

Dateline said it had never offered to pay anyone for a sound bite and was not working on a story about 911 calls. [S3]

Six days later, on August 16, at 12:08 p.m., Hupp called 911 from her home in O'Fallon. Someone had broken in, she said, and she had shot him. [S3]

## The note

His name was Louis Gumpenberger. He was 33, and he lived with mental and physical disabilities from an earlier accident. [S3] [S4]

Hupp told police he was a stranger who had held a knife on her and demanded she take him to a bank "to get Russ's money." She said she got a gun and shot him. [S4]

Police found $900 and a handwritten note in his pockets. According to the probable cause statement, the note appeared to instruct him to kidnap Hupp, get Russ Faria's money from her bank, and kill her to collect the rest of $10,000. The name "Faria" was on it. [S3]

Four of the $100 bills he carried had serial numbers in sequence with a $100 bill found in Hupp's possession. [S2]

There was money tied to the Faria name in Pam Hupp's life. It was Betsy's life insurance. [S1]

Hupp had said she put $100,000 of it in trust for Betsy's daughters. In a deposition for a lawsuit those daughters brought, she admitted she had kept all of it. [S1]

Prosecutors said Hupp had lured Gumpenberger to her house by posing as a Dateline producer. They said she was trying to make it look as though Russ had hired a hit man to kill her. [S3] [S4] She was charged with first-degree murder and armed criminal action on August 23, 2016. [S3]

That case never went to trial. Hupp entered an Alford plea in June 2019, meaning she did not admit guilt but agreed the evidence was strong enough to convict her. She was sentenced to life without parole. [S1]

Two years later, Lincoln County filed a new murder charge in Betsy's death. This time, it didn't name Russ. [S1]

## Where it stands

Lincoln County Prosecuting Attorney Mike Wood charged Pam Hupp with first-degree murder in Betsy's death in July 2021. [S1] [S4] Prosecutors allege she killed Betsy for the life insurance money and staged the scene to make Russ look guilty. [S4] Hupp has pleaded not guilty. [S5]

![Pam Hupp in a booking photo, wearing an orange jail uniform](./betsy-faria-2011-pam-hupp.jpg "Pam Hupp in a booking photo. Image: KSDK")

The case was moved to Springfield, about four hours away. Wood dropped and refiled the charge in October 2023 so he could seek a trial closer to home. He said the long trip was "too much to ask of witnesses" and of Betsy's family. [S4]

Hupp gave up her right to a jury trial in April 2026. In exchange, prosecutors agreed not to seek the death penalty. Russ has said he supports the decision. A judge is scheduled to hear the case starting January 31, 2028. [S5]

In September 2026, Hupp's lawyers asked the court to keep out all evidence about Louis Gumpenberger's death. Joel Schwartz, the attorney who won Russ's acquittal, said he expects prosecutors to argue that it shows "consciousness of guilt." A hearing is set for October 19, 2026. [S6]

Russ has pointed out that in a bench trial, the judge hears everything. He is waiting to testify, something he says he hasn't done in 15 years. [S6]

Pam Hupp has not been tried for Betsy's death and is presumed innocent of that charge. No one stands convicted of killing her. [S5]

Betsy Faria has been gone for nearly 15 years. The daughters who were teenagers when she died are grown now. It was in their lawsuit that Hupp admitted she had kept the insurance money. [S1] [S2]

Before the trial begins, a judge will decide whether to hear about a man who died in O'Fallon nearly five years after Betsy died in Troy. [S6] Should Louis Gumpenberger's death be part of the case for Betsy's?
