At about 11 p.m. on December 9, 2001, Pamela Dunn called her mother. She said David Asmussen, her former boyfriend, had called her, and that he sounded upset, maybe like he was crying. [S2]

It is the last time anyone is known to have heard from her. [S2] She was due at work the next morning, and she never got there. [S2]

When police got into her Watertown apartment that day, the door was locked and her purse was on the table. [S7, S10] Her car was still in the lot. [S4] Five years later, a jury convicted Asmussen of kidnapping her, and he is serving life in prison. [S3, S4] He maintains he is innocent. [S8] Pam has never been found. [S10]

This December marks 25 years since she disappeared. [S1] A man is in prison for kidnapping her. The question her family has asked ever since is the one no court has answered: where is she? [S6]

## Pam

Pamela Jean Dunn was born on October 4, 1963, and she was 38 when she disappeared. She was 5 feet 2, about 135 pounds, with brown hair and brown eyes. [S1] She worked at Jenkins Living Center in Watertown. [S9] Her coworkers, Forum News Service reported in 2022, knew her as reliable. [S8] She had a daughter, and she had a dog. [S2, S7]

![Pamela Dunn, smiling, with curled brown hair and star-shaped earrings](./pamela-dunn-2001-portrait.jpg "Pamela Dunn in an undated photo.")

Deb Tarnowski was her best friend before the two of them learned they were half sisters. After that, Forum reported, they looked out for each other. [S8] As teenagers they would go to a park in Watertown together. "We used to come here just for peace and just to hang out and be kids again," Deb told KELOLAND News in 2020. [S5]

That is most of what the public record says about who Pam was. Deb started a Facebook page in 2012, "In Memory of Pamela Dunn," and asked anyone who knew Pam to share their stories there. [S6]

## The protection order

Pam and Asmussen had a long relationship that ended sometime in August 2000. [S2] Deb told Forum she knew almost right away that he didn't deserve her sister. "Something didn't sit right with me about him, but I couldn't put my thumb on it," she said. "But, it was her life and I thought, if he makes her happy, more power to them. But things started getting really rocky right away, and everyone kept telling her to get rid of him." [S8] The two broke up and got back together many times. At one point she accepted his proposal. [S7, S8]

On August 25, 2000, a Codington County court issued a protection order against Asmussen. It barred him from contacting Pam in any way, directly or not, and from contacting her daughter, her mother, the rest of her family, her employer and her coworkers. He wasn't to come within 100 feet of any vehicle she owned. The order ran until August 2003, and across the top of its first page it said, "VIOLATION OF THIS PROTECTION ORDER IS A CRIMINAL OFFENSE." [S2]

He called anyway. According to the South Dakota Supreme Court, phone records show he made 17 calls to her home phone between November 25 and December 9, 2001. [S2] Forum, citing court documents, puts the number at 41 calls in the month before she disappeared. [S8]

Three threatening and abusive messages were saved on her voice mail. The caller didn't give his name. [S2] In one, according to the court documents Forum reviewed, the caller says she will get "the full effect" for seeing someone new, and that she will "get the consequences for the rest of your life." [S8] At trial, the state identified the caller as Asmussen. [S2]

## The last calls

On the evening of December 9, around 8 p.m., Pam's daughter called her to ask whether she had a phone number for someone they knew. Pam said she didn't, but that Asmussen would. While they were on the phone, Pam talked with Asmussen in the background. She explained that he was there picking up the last of his things. [S2]

Then came the 11 p.m. call to her mother. [S2] The missing poster lists December 9 as the day she went missing, and later news reports give December 10, the day she didn't come to work. [S1, S4] Both are true to the record. December 9 is the last day anyone heard from her, and December 10 is the day people noticed she was gone. [S2]

## The apartment

When Pam didn't show up for her shift on the morning of December 10, a coworker drove to her apartment. Her car was in the parking lot. As the coworker got to the door, she could hear a television that sounded like it was turned all the way up. [S7]

Watertown police were called for a welfare check, and a maintenance worker let officers in. [S7] What they found is set out in the affidavit police filed to support his arrest, as Forum described it in 2022. The phone cord had been ripped out of the wall, and a caller ID box was gone. Her keys, cash, checkbook, prescription medicine and winter coat were all still there. So was her dog. The one thing that seemed to be missing was the engagement ring from Asmussen. [S7]

The door had been bolted, and her keys were inside. [S7] The only other person with a key, Forum reported, was Asmussen. [S7, S8] Two days later, police brought in bloodhounds. They called the disappearance unusual, because her car was still parked outside. [S4]

## His account

Forum reported that Asmussen came to the Watertown Police Department on "the day of Dunn's disappearance," without saying whether that meant December 9 or December 10, and denied having anything to do with it. [S7]

At first, the court documents say, he denied being at her apartment the night before. Then he told officers he had been there from about 6:30 to 8:30 p.m., that the two had argued and then made up, and that he had spent the night at his trailer in Bemis, about 20 minutes away. [S7] The state Supreme Court records that he admitted being at her home and talking to her on the phone on December 8 and 9. [S2]

## The blue Cavalier

Investigators also tracked the vehicles he drove on December 9 and 10. According to Forum's account of the court documents, his story about them didn't match what other people told police. On the evening of December 9 he picked up a blue Chevy Cavalier from a friend. The next morning he left it at an associate's shop, where, the associate said, the two of them took off the license plate. [S7] The car was sold before police could inspect it. When they did search it, they found hair consistent with Pam's in color and length, but DNA testing couldn't show it was hers. [S7]

After December 9, the court documents show, his calls to her stopped. [S8]

What stood out to investigators, Forum reported, was his lack of concern for Pam herself. [S7] "You'd think that a guy whose love of his life has gone missing, that he'd be worried about finding her. He was worried about defending himself," Vincent Foley, then the Codington County state's attorney, told Forum. [S7]

## Two trials

On February 7, 2002, police arrested Asmussen, accused of stalking Pam on two separate days. They didn't call him a suspect in her disappearance. [S4] He pleaded not guilty that October. [S4]

![David Asmussen, in glasses and a striped jail uniform, seated in a courtroom](./pamela-dunn-2001-asmussen-court.jpg "David Asmussen in court.")

Prosecutors went after the stalking case first. "The goal was to get him locked up and then proceed to work the bigger case. And we were able to do that," Foley told Forum. [S8]

The stalking trial was held in Huron on August 23, 2004. Three days earlier, Asmussen had dismissed his lawyers so he could represent himself. [S2] Jurors heard parts of the messages from December 5 and 6, and Jon Bierne, an agent with the state Division of Criminal Investigation, testified that in his opinion the voice was Asmussen's. Asmussen made no opening statement and no closing argument. [S2] The jury found him guilty of two counts of stalking, and he was sentenced to 40 months in prison. [S2] The South Dakota Supreme Court upheld the conviction in April 2006. [S2]

That May, he was back in a Watertown courtroom, this time charged with kidnapping her. [S4] The court ordered a mental evaluation before he could represent himself. [S4] He was found competent, and once again he acted as his own lawyer at trial. [S3] At several points he tried to raise a defense based on the Uniform Commercial Code, which the Supreme Court later called "nonsensical." The trial judge rejected it. [S3]

![David Asmussen, in a striped jail uniform, is led through a courthouse by uniformed officers](./pamela-dunn-2001-asmussen-escorted.jpg "David Asmussen is led through a courthouse by officers.")

With Pam still missing, prosecutors had to prove at least that he had taken Pam from her apartment against her will, Forum reported. [S8] The ring was part of their case. Asmussen's sister-in-law at the time gave it to investigators and told them he had dropped it off at her house before his 2004 sentencing, according to court documents. [S8]

On December 12, 2006, a Watertown jury convicted him. [S4] The conviction was for two counts of first-degree kidnapping. [S3] He was sentenced to life in prison. [S3] KELOLAND reported the sentence as life without parole, and gives the sentencing date as January 10, 2007. [S4] The state Supreme Court says he was sentenced in December 2006 and that the judgment was filed on January 10. [S3]

Asmussen still maintains he is innocent, Forum reported in 2022. [S8]

## The well

The search for Pam didn't stop with the verdict. In 2017, acting on a tip, authorities used a vacuum truck to search a well on an abandoned farm in Deuel County. They found human hair, but it was too degraded to test for DNA. [S5, S8]

On November 4, 2020, the state Division of Criminal Investigation, Watertown police and the Deuel County Sheriff's Office went back and dug out the well. [S4, S5] Deb told KELOLAND the news left her torn. "I'm hoping just for closure, but then there's part of me saying 'No, you know, hopefully something else will be found,'" she said. "It's my life's mission to find answers and find what happened to her." [S5]

Most of what they dug up was animal remains. A few pieces were sent off for testing. [S5] In 2021, Capt. Chad Stahl of the Watertown Police Department said the bones they dug up weren't human. [S6] "We went for the full excavation of the well because we wanted to make sure," he told Forum in 2022. "Unfortunately, it didn't pan out the way we wanted it to." [S8]

## The motion

Asmussen never filed a direct appeal of the kidnapping conviction. [S3] He challenged it in a habeas petition in January 2015. At the end of 2018, a judge dismissed it because it was filed too late, about six months past the deadline, the state Supreme Court said. In 2019, that court refused to let him appeal. [S3]

In June 2025, his lawyer, Ryan Kolbeck, filed a motion asking the court to correct what he called an illegal sentence. [S9, S10] The motion says the written judgment wrongly describes a voluntary guilty plea, when in fact a jury convicted him. It also says the judge imposed only one life sentence, so there aren't two sentences to run at the same time. Kolbeck called the judgment "self-contradictory." [S9, S10]

KWAT reported that a hearing was set for September 22, 2025. [S9] In early October, KELOLAND reported that, as of that Wednesday, no hearings were scheduled. [S10] No public report of a ruling was found for this story.

In an email to Pam's family, which KELOLAND obtained, Codington County State's Attorney Alison Bakken wrote, "I've made it clear to Dave and his attorney that I will not stop looking for her and investigating this case." [S10]

## Where it stands

This story's case status was last checked on October 21, 2026. Asmussen is serving life for kidnapping Pam. [S3, S10] His motion to correct the sentence was the most recent court action found, and no ruling on it has been reported. [S9, S10]

The record documents a lot. A protection order was in place, and he called her anyway. [S2] He was at her apartment the night of December 9. [S2] The next day, according to Forum's account of the affidavit, the door was bolted, her keys and coat were inside, and her engagement ring appeared to be gone. [S7] Two juries convicted him, first of stalking her and then of kidnapping her. [S2, S3]

What the record doesn't show is what happened to Pam after that night. Kidnapping is the only crime Asmussen has been convicted of in her case, and he says he didn't take her. [S3, S8] Searches have turned up hair that couldn't be tested and bones that weren't hers. [S5, S6]

Watertown police say they still follow up on any leads they get. [S6] Stahl has said he hopes Asmussen will one day tell them where she is. "I don't know if he will ever do that," he told KELOLAND in 2021. "In domestic relationships and situations like that, it's a power and control thing." [S6] Deb said much the same to Forum in 2022. "It doesn't give us closure," she said of the conviction. "It's the last piece of control that he has." [S8]
