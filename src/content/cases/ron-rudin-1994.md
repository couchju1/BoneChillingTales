A scuba diver found it at the bottom of Lake Mead, wrapped in plastic bags. [S1]

A .22 caliber Ruger pistol with a silencer attached. [S1]

The gun had been registered to a Las Vegas developer named Ron Rudin in 1980. He had reported it missing in 1988. [S1]

Ron collected guns. [S2] This one had been missing for six years before he died. [S1]

By the time it came up out of the water, in July 1996, Ron had been dead for a year and a half. He had been shot in the head several times with a .22. [S1]

Someone wrapped that gun and put it in the lake. No court has ever been able to say who. [S1] [S4]

## A city going up

Las Vegas in the 1980s and early 1990s was a city under construction. Ron Rudin rode the boom into real estate and became a successful developer and a powerful figure in town. [S2]

He kept his world close. His realty company sat in a strip mall he owned, and his house stood right behind it. [S1]

People who knew him said he had charm and knew how to use it. In a town built on gambling, he stayed out of the casinos. [S2]

He wore slick black cowboy boots. He collected guns. He kept his Cadillac spotless. [S2]

His newest venture in 1994 was an RV resort called The Retreat at Lee Canyon. [S2]

Ron had been married before. His third wife, Peggy, died of a gunshot wound in their home, and authorities ruled her death a suicide. [S2]

He met Margaret at church, and they married in September 1987. [S1] [S2] Margaret had an antique store of her own. [S1]

![Ron Rudin and Margaret Rudin relaxing together at home](./ron-rudin-1994-at-home.jpg "Ron and Margaret Rudin at home.")

Four years into the marriage, Ron went to his lawyer with something he wanted kept secret. [S1]

## The week before Christmas

It was a Sunday evening, one week before Christmas, 1994. [S1]

A tenant at the strip mall saw Ron walking toward Margaret's antique store. [S1]

Around 8:10 p.m., a friend of Margaret's called the house and spoke with Ron. As far as the public record shows, that was the last time anyone heard from him. [S1]

Ron opened the realty office on Monday mornings. That Monday, it stayed closed. [S1]

The next day, two of his employees went to the police and reported him missing. [S1]

Five days after that phone call, police found his Cadillac. It was locked, parked in an alley behind the Crazy Horse Too Saloon. [S1]

Dirt had been tracked onto all four floorboards. [S1]

Investigators lifted several fingerprints from the car. None belonged to Ron. None belonged to Margaret. [S1]

For a month, that car was the only piece of Ron Rudin anyone could find.

## Nelson's Landing

Fishermen found him at night on January 21, 1995, near Nelson's Landing at Lake Mohave. The remains had been burned. Dental records confirmed who he was. [S1]

He had been shot in the head several times. With him were the burned remains of a large steamer trunk. [S1]

He had been missing for 34 days. [S1]

Investigators now had a body, a burned trunk, and a car with dirt on every floor. They turned to the house behind the strip mall.

## What the house held

A police search found tiny splatters of what appeared to be blood on the bedroom walls and ceiling. [S1]

A day laborer later testified that in January 1995, Margaret had him tear out the bedroom carpet, which he said had dark stains, and haul the mattress away. [S1]

Margaret's sister testified she had seen a trunk like the burned one in Margaret's antique shop. An antique dealer recalled selling her one. [S1]

While police searched the house on January 27, 1995, Margaret left Nevada. [S1]

Then there was what Ron had put in writing.

According to testimony summarized by the Nevada Supreme Court, Ron told his lawyer in 1991 that Margaret was becoming "vicious and violent." He wrote a secret instruction: if he died by violent means, his trustees should investigate. [S1]

Two years later, he raised her share of his trust from 40 to 60 percent. [S1] His estate was worth at least $8 million. Prosecutors would argue she killed him for that money. [S3]

Margaret has always said she did not kill her husband. [S3] She has said investigators were so focused on her that they missed other motives, including the Lee Canyon project. Her lawyer has raised questions about where that project's money came from. [S2]

First, though, someone had to find her.

## A pizza in Revere

A grand jury indicted Margaret for murder on April 17, 1997. [S1] By then she was gone, moving from place to place under different names. [S2]

Nearly five years after she left Nevada, a tip came in. She was mailing packages from a post office in Revere, Massachusetts. [S1] [S2]

Police there arrested her in November 1999. [S1] One of the officers who helped make the arrest was posing as a pizza delivery man. [S2]

![Margaret Rudin with her glasses pushed up on her head, resting her hand on her forehead](./ron-rudin-1994-margaret.jpg "Margaret Rudin.")

She would face a jury. What happened in that courtroom would be argued over for more than twenty years. [S1] [S3]

## Six days

The trial began March 2, 2001. Her lead lawyer was Michael Amador. In the first days of the trial, Margaret told the judge he wasn't prepared. [S1]

The Nevada Supreme Court would later quote his opening statement at length. Meanwhile, prosecutors were looking into reports that he had taken money from media companies for her story. [S1]

When the jury went out, one juror held out for not guilty. She held out for six days. [S2]

Then she gave in. She later said it was the biggest regret of her life. [S2]

The verdict came on May 2, 2001: guilty of first-degree murder. Margaret was sentenced to life in prison, with a chance at parole after 20 years. [S2]

Over the years she turned down five plea deals. She said she would not plead guilty to something she didn't do. [S2]

The judge who presided, Joseph Bonaventure, later told ABC News: "In 42 years, that is the worst trial that I've ever had." [S2]

![Margaret Rudin seated in a courtroom, looking down](./ron-rudin-1994-court.jpg "Margaret Rudin in court.")

## Undone

The Nevada Supreme Court upheld the conviction in 2004, over a dissent that would have ordered a new trial. The majority said questions about her lawyer belonged in a separate proceeding. [S1]

Margaret served 20 years. She was released on parole on January 10, 2020. [S2]

Two years later, U.S. District Judge Richard Boulware threw out her conviction. He found she had been denied effective counsel and called Amador's work at trial "self-absorbed, ill-prepared, and incompetent." Amador had since died. [S3]

The judge wrote something else, too. He found no evidence linking Margaret to the murder weapon, to Ron's abandoned car, or to the suspected crime scene. [S5]

The state chose not to try her again. [S5] A Clark County judge dismissed the murder charges with prejudice on December 9, 2024, so she can never be tried for them again. [S4]

"Margaret has never been convicted legally of any crimes and she can never be re-prosecuted," her lawyer, Adam Breeden, said. [S4]

The state never said she was innocent. It agreed she'd had poor legal representation. [S5]

![Margaret Rudin, with short gray hair, standing in front of a brick wall](./ron-rudin-1994-margaret-recent.jpg "Margaret Rudin in a recent photo.")

## The prints in the Cadillac

Go back to that alley behind the Crazy Horse Too, two days before Christmas 1994. [S1]

A locked Cadillac. Dirt on every floorboard. Fingerprints that belonged to neither Ron nor Margaret. [S1]

For three decades the case turned on Margaret: the trunk, the carpet, her flight, her trial.

Now a federal judge has written that nothing tied her to that car. Margaret says she doesn't know who killed her husband. Her lawyers say a fingerprint could be the key to finding out. [S5]

Ron Rudin once asked, in writing, for an investigation if he died by violence. He got one. It has never ended in a conviction that stood. [S1] [S3] [S4]

## Where it stands

Margaret sued Nevada in 2024 for wrongful conviction, seeking compensation and a certificate of innocence. [S6] A judge dismissed the suit without prejudice on January 6, 2026, so it can be filed again. Her lawyer said it ended without any ruling on the merits, for confidential reasons. [S5] [S6]

No one stands convicted of killing Ron Rudin. [S4]
