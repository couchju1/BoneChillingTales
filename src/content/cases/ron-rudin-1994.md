## Who Ron Rudin was

Ron Rudin made his money as Las Vegas grew. When construction boomed in the 1980s and 1990s, he became a successful real estate developer and a powerful figure in town. [S2] He owned a realty company in a strip mall he also owned, and he lived in a house right behind it. [S1]

People who knew him said he had charm and knew how to use it. He stayed away from the casinos. He wore slick black cowboy boots, collected guns, and kept his Cadillac spotless. [S2] In 1994 he was working on an RV resort project called The Retreat at Lee Canyon. [S2]

His third wife, Peggy, died of a gunshot wound in their home. Authorities ruled her death a suicide. [S2] He met Margaret at church, and they married in September 1987. [S1] [S2]

## December 18, 1994

On the evening of Sunday, December 18, 1994, a tenant at the strip mall saw Ron walking toward Margaret's antique store. Around 8:10 p.m., a friend of Margaret's called the house and spoke to Ron. [S1]

The next morning he didn't open his realty office, as he usually did on Mondays. On December 20, two of his employees went to police to report him missing. [S1]

On December 23, his car was found locked in an alley behind the Crazy Horse Too Saloon. Dirt had been tracked onto all four floorboards. Investigators lifted several fingerprints from the car. None belonged to Ron or Margaret. [S1]

## What investigators found

On the night of January 21, 1995, fishermen found burned remains near Nelson's Landing at Lake Mohave. Dental records showed they were Ron's. He had been shot in the head several times with a .22 caliber gun. The burned remains of a large steamer trunk were found with him. [S1]

On July 21, 1996, a scuba diver found a .22 caliber Ruger pistol with a silencer in Lake Mead, wrapped in plastic bags. It had been registered to Ron in 1980. He had reported it missing in 1988. [S1]

## The case against Margaret Rudin

Prosecutors argued that Margaret killed Ron for his money. [S3] His estate was worth at least $8 million. [S3] According to testimony summarized by the Nevada Supreme Court:

- In 1991, Ron told his lawyer that Margaret was becoming "vicious and violent," and wrote a secret instruction that his trustees investigate if he died by violent means. Two years later, he raised her share of his trust from 40 to 60 percent. [S1]
- A day laborer testified that in January 1995 she had him tear out the bedroom carpet, which he said had dark stains, and haul away the mattress. [S1]
- A police search of the house found tiny splatters of what appeared to be blood on the bedroom walls and ceiling. [S1]
- Her sister testified she had seen a trunk like the burned one in Margaret's antique shop. An antique dealer recalled selling her one. [S1]

Margaret has always said she did not kill her husband. [S3] She has said investigators were so focused on her that they overlooked other motives, including the Lee Canyon project. Her lawyer has raised questions about where that project's money came from. [S2]

## Two years on the run

On January 27, 1995, while police searched her house, Margaret left Nevada. [S1] A grand jury indicted her for murder on April 17, 1997. [S1] By then she was a fugitive, moving between places under different names. [S2]

In November 1999, police in Massachusetts tracked her to Revere, after a tip that she was mailing packages from the post office there. [S1] [S2] They arrested her with the help of an officer posing as a pizza delivery man. [S2]

## The trial

The trial began on March 2, 2001. [S1] Her lead lawyer, Michael Amador, gave an opening statement the Nevada Supreme Court later quoted at length, and Margaret told the judge in the first days of the trial that he wasn't prepared. [S1] Prosecutors were also looking into reports that Amador had taken money from media companies for her story. [S1]

One juror held out for a not guilty verdict for six days. She later said that giving in was the biggest regret of her life. [S2] On May 2, 2001, the jury found Margaret guilty of first-degree murder. She was sentenced to life in prison with a chance of parole after 20 years. [S2] Over the years she turned down five plea deals, saying she wouldn't plead guilty to something she didn't do. [S2]

The trial judge, Joseph Bonaventure, later told ABC News: "In 42 years, that is the worst trial that I've ever had." [S2]

## A conviction undone

The Nevada Supreme Court upheld the conviction in 2004, over a dissent that would have ordered a new trial. The majority said concerns about her lawyer had to be examined in a separate proceeding. [S1] Margaret was released on parole on January 10, 2020, after 20 years in prison. [S2]

In May 2022, U.S. District Judge Richard Boulware threw out her conviction. He found she had been denied effective help from her lawyer, citing Amador's "self-absorbed, ill-prepared, and incompetent" work at trial. [S3] Amador had since died. [S3] The judge also wrote that there was no evidence linking her to the murder weapon, Ron's abandoned car, or the suspected crime scene. [S5]

The state and the Clark County District Attorney's Office chose not to try her again. [S5] On December 9, 2024, a Clark County judge dismissed the murder charges with prejudice, which means she can never be tried for them again. [S4] "Margaret has never been convicted legally of any crimes and she can never be re-prosecuted," her lawyer, Adam Breeden, said. [S4]

The state did not admit she was innocent. It agreed she had poor legal representation at trial. [S5]

## Where it stands

Margaret sued Nevada in 2024 for wrongful conviction, asking for compensation and a certificate of innocence. [S6] On January 6, 2026, a judge dismissed that lawsuit without prejudice, so it can be filed again. Her lawyer said it ended without any ruling on the merits, for confidential reasons. [S5] [S6]

She has said she doesn't know who killed her husband. Her lawyers have said a fingerprint could be the key to finding out. [S5]

No one stands convicted of killing Ron Rudin. [S4] If you know something about his death, contact the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department.
