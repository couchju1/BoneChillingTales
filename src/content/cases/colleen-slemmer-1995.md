## Who Colleen was

Colleen Slemmer grew up in Orange Park, Florida. [S4] Her mother, May Martinez, has described her as very active. She ran, roller skated every weekend and called herself a "computer geek." [S5] She volunteered with the Special Olympics. [S10]

At 19 she moved to Knoxville, Tennessee, to train at the Job Corps center there. [S1] Job Corps is a federal program that helps young people finish school and learn a trade.

## The night of January 12

Three other Job Corps students would be convicted in her death. Christa Pike was 18. Her boyfriend, Tadaryl Shipp, was 17. Shadolla Peterson was 18. [S1] [S7]

According to the court record, Pike believed Colleen was interested in Shipp, and the two women didn't get along. [S1] On January 11, 1995, Pike told another student she planned to kill Colleen. [S1]

The next evening, Colleen left the center with Pike, Shipp and Peterson. They walked to an isolated spot near the University of Tennessee's agricultural campus. [S1] Pike, Shipp and Peterson came back later that night. Colleen did not. [S1]

Pike later told police what happened there. In her recorded statement, she said Colleen was beaten and cut, and was stopped each time she tried to get away. [S1] Pike said Colleen begged them to let her go and offered to go back to Florida. [S1] Pike estimated the attack lasted 30 minutes to an hour. [S1] The medical examiner found that Colleen died of blunt force injuries to her head. [S1]

## What investigators found

The next morning, January 13, a university employee found Colleen's body near the campus greenhouses. [S1]

Pike had already started talking. Classmates testified that she described the killing to them, and that she showed one of them a small piece of bone she said came from Colleen's skull. [S1] Investigators found a bone fragment in the pocket of Pike's jacket. A forensic examination matched it to Colleen. [S1]

On January 14, Pike waived her right to stay silent and gave police a recorded statement. She also took investigators to the scene. [S1] Blood on clothing belonging to Pike and Shipp matched Colleen. [S1]

## The trial

Pike's trial was held in Knox County in March 1996. Her lawyers didn't dispute that she killed Colleen. They argued she hadn't planned it. [S1]

On March 22, 1996, the jury convicted Pike of premeditated first-degree murder and conspiracy to commit first-degree murder. [S1] The jury sentenced her to death. It found two reasons the law requires for a death sentence: the killing involved torture or serious physical abuse beyond what it took to cause death, and it was done to keep Colleen from reporting the attack. [S1] The judge added 25 years for the conspiracy. [S1]

## The other two

Tadaryl Shipp was tried separately. He was convicted of first-degree murder and conspiracy and sentenced to life in prison plus 25 years. [S3] Because he was 17 at the time, he could not be sentenced to death. [S7] In October 2025, the Tennessee parole board denied him parole, citing how serious the crime was. His next review is set for October 2031. [S3]

Shadolla Peterson pleaded guilty as an accessory and was sentenced to probation. [S7]

## Thirty years in court

In 1998 the Tennessee Supreme Court upheld Pike's conviction and death sentence. [S1]

Her later appeals centered on her childhood. Her lawyers presented evidence that she was sexually abused as a young child and as a teenager, neglected and moved between homes, and that the jury never heard most of it. [S8] In 2019 a federal appeals court ruled against her. It found the new evidence was mostly a repeat of what jurors had heard, and not enough to outweigh the nature of the crime. [S2]

She was 18 when Colleen was killed. The U.S. Supreme Court bars the death penalty for crimes committed before 18, so the rule did not cover her. [S8]

## September 30, 2026

Pike was scheduled to be the first woman executed in Tennessee in more than 200 years. [S10] The execution began on the night of September 30, 2026, after a day of last-minute court challenges. [S5]

It failed. Executioners struggled to place the IV lines, and two doses of the drug did not end her life. [S5] [S6] The execution was called off, and Pike was taken to a hospital. [S8]

Governor Bill Lee ordered an independent review of what went wrong and stopped the rest of the executions planned for 2026. [S5] A judge ordered the state to preserve evidence from the attempt. [S9]

May Martinez traveled from Florida to Nashville to be there. She said afterward that the state owes her an apology. [S5] For thirty years she has asked people to remember her daughter, not the woman who killed her. [S4]

## Where it stands

Christa Pike's death sentence still stands. She is alive, and the fight over what happened on September 30 has moved to the courts. [S9] The governor's pause covers the rest of the executions scheduled for 2026. [S5]

Tadaryl Shipp remains in prison. [S3]
