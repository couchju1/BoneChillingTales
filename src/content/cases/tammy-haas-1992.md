On September 23, 1992, a golfer went looking for lost balls near Crofton, Nebraska [S2].

The golfer found Tammy Haas instead [S2].

She was 19 [S1]. She lived in Yankton, South Dakota, just across the river [S16]. She had gone to a party near Crofton several days earlier, and she did not come home that night [S1].

The FBI's poster says her body was found in a ditch near where the party had been [S1]. Newspaper accounts describe a ravine near the Crofton Lakeview Golf Course [S3][S4][S8].

More than three decades later, there has been an investigation, a manslaughter charge, a trial, and an acquittal [S16]. There has been a federal reward [S1].

And the FBI still lists her case under two words: Unexplained Death [S1].

* * *

Tamara Ann Haas was born on April 13, 1973 [S1]. Everyone called her Tammy [S1].

In September 1992, Yankton High School was in the middle of homecoming, according to the Yankton Press & Dakotan [S4][S8]. Her mother was Nancy Haas [S3]. Her brother was Paul [S3]. Her boyfriend was Eric Stukel [S3]. (NBC spells his first name Erick [S2].)

The public record doesn't say much about Tammy's everyday life that fall. Where she worked, what she was planning, what made her laugh. Those memories belong to her family and the people who grew up with her.

What the record gives us is one September night, and the long silence after it.

* * *

On September 17, 1992, Tammy went to a party at a farm near Crofton with her boyfriend [S1][S3]. The Norfolk Daily News places the farm in Cedar County, Nebraska [S16]. The Associated Press reports that September 17 was the last day she was seen [S3].

The next day, she was reported missing, according to the Norfolk Daily News [S16].

That's nearly everything the public record says about the hours in between. It doesn't establish when Tammy left the farm, where she went next, or the last time anyone saw her alive.

The FBI's account is short. She went to the party. She did not return home that night [S1].

Then came the waiting.

On September 23, the golfer found her [S2][S3].

* * *

An autopsy gave investigators part of an answer.

The FBI says the autopsy showed her death was caused by "hyperflexation of her neck" [S1]. The 2021 Yankton Press & Dakotan described investigators' finding as "a severe blow to the neck or a hyperflexion of the neck" [S8].

Those descriptions tell us what kind of injury it was. They don't tell us who or what caused it, where it happened, or when.

There was one more detail, and it stays with you.

According to NBC, authorities at the time believed Tammy had been in the trunk of a car before she was left where she was found [S2]. That was an investigative assessment as reported, not a finding proven in court. The reporting doesn't say which car.

It raises the questions the record still can't answer. Where was Tammy after the party? And how did she come to be in that place by the golf course?

* * *

In October 1995, three years after Tammy died, Eric Stukel was charged with manslaughter [S16]. According to the Norfolk Daily News, the charge came three days before the statute of limitations ran out [S16].

The case was brought in Cedar County [S16]. The Associated Press reported that the Nebraska attorney general's office prosecuted it [S3].

The 2005 Yankton Press & Dakotan places the trial in Hartington, Nebraska, in 1996 [S4]. The Norfolk Daily News also says he was tried a year after the charge [S16]. (A 2021 Press & Dakotan article says 1995 [S8]. Court records would settle it.)

Stukel was represented by Yankton attorney Mike Stevens [S4].

The jury acquitted him [S4][S16].

That verdict is the final word from the courts on the charge. What the jury heard, and why it decided the way it did, is not something we could verify from the public record, and we won't guess.

* * *

The verdict didn't close anything for either family.

In 2005, a Yankton author published a novel based on the case [S4]. Stevens, who told the Press & Dakotan he hadn't seen the book, said the verdict "doesn't end the situation" for the family [S4]. He criticized "people who are unwilling to accept the judgment of 12 unbiased (jurors)" [S4]. Those were comments to a reporter nine years after the trial, not arguments he made in court.

In 2015, Tammy's family pushed the case back into the light. They raised about $24,000 through social media and hired private investigators, according to the AP [S3]. That April, Dateline NBC featured Tammy in its Cold Case Spotlight [S3].

One of Tammy's childhood friends, Sally Smith, told Dateline she believes there was a cover-up [S2]. That's her personal belief. No investigation has established it.

Even the coverage got tangled that spring. The AP had to correct a story that put a line defending Stukel, "There's no way I doubt his innocence," in the mouth of Tammy's mother [S3]. It was actually said by Dawn Stukel, Eric Stukel's mother [S3].

Two mothers. Two families. Both still living with that September.

* * *

Then, in 2021, the case took a turn that's quietly unsettling once you sit with it.

On April 13, 2021, the FBI and Yankton Police held a news conference in Yankton [S7][S8]. It was the day Tammy would have turned 48 [S1][S8].

They announced a reward of up to $15,000 [S1][S7].

Not for an arrest. Not for a conviction. The FBI's poster asks for information uncovering *exactly how* Tammy Haas died [S1].

Michael Paul, the FBI special agent in charge, put the question plainly: "What happened that night? Why did she die?" [S8]

Nearly thirty years after a golfer found her, after a charge and a jury trial, the official question still isn't "who." It's "how."

The FBI and Yankton Police said they were continuing to investigate and hoped the reward would bring people forward [S7]. As of October 10, 2026, the FBI's listing for Tammy is still online, and it still lists the reward [S1].

* * *

Here is where things stand.

**What's documented:** Tammy Haas, 19, of Yankton, went to a party near Crofton, Nebraska, on September 17, 1992 [S1][S3]. Her body was found on September 23 [S3]. The FBI says an autopsy attributed her death to hyperflexation of her neck [S1]. Eric Stukel was charged with manslaughter in October 1995 and acquitted by a jury [S16]. In 2021, the FBI and Yankton Police offered up to $15,000 for information about how she died [S1][S7].

**Where the sources disagree:** The FBI says ditch. Newspapers say ravine [S1][S3][S4]. Sources give the trial year as 1996 or 1995 [S4][S16][S8].

**What's reported but not proven:** Authorities believed she had been in a car trunk, according to NBC [S2]. A childhood friend believes there was a cover-up [S2].

**What no one has publicly answered:** When Tammy left the party. When and where she was injured. How she came to be where she was found.

Investigators said in 2021 that they hoped the reward would bring people with information forward [S7]. More than thirty years on, that information may still be out there.
